A computable lower bound for coercivity

The residual error bound divides the residual dual norm by a positive certified lower bound \(\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}(\mu)\). This page constructs one such bound and states exactly when the inexpensive min-Theta formula is valid.

What must be bounded?

Fix a state norm \(\|\cdot\|_V\), independent of the parameter. For a coercive bilinear form, its best coercivity constant is

\[\alpha(\mu)=\inf_{v\in V_h\setminus\{0\}} \frac{a_\mu(v,v)}{\|v\|_V^2}>0.\]

We need a number that is guaranteed to satisfy

\[0<\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}(\mu)\leq\alpha(\mu) \qquad\text{for every admissible }\mu.\]

For a discrete matrix \(A(\mu)\) and fixed positive-definite Gram matrix \(G\), this is the smallest generalized eigenvalue of the symmetric part \(A_s(\mu)=(A(\mu)+A(\mu)^T)/2\) relative to \(G\):

\[\alpha(\mu)=\min_{\mathbf v\ne0} \frac{\mathbf v^TA_s(\mu)\mathbf v}{\mathbf v^TG\mathbf v}.\]

Solving this full-dimensional eigenproblem at every new parameter would spoil a cheap online prediction. A lower bound may be less sharp, provided its inequality is proved in the same norm used for the residual dual norm.

The min-Theta construction

Suppose the affine decomposition is

\[a_\mu(w,v)=\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\Theta_q(\mu)a_q(w,v).\]

Choose a reference parameter \(\bar\mu\). The following sufficient hypotheses make the construction valid:

  • Each component is nonnegative on the diagonal: \(a_q(v,v)\geq0\) for every \(v\in V_h\). Individual components may be semidefinite; they need not each be coercive.

  • The coefficient functions satisfy \(\Theta_q(\mu)\geq0\) throughout the parameter domain. For each term active at the reference, \(\Theta_q(\bar\mu)>0\).

  • The reference form is coercive in the declared norm, and a verified \(\bar\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}>0\) satisfies \(\bar\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}\leq\alpha(\bar\mu)\).

Let \(J_{\bar\mu}=\{q:\Theta_q(\bar\mu)>0\}\) and define

\[c(\mu;\bar\mu) =\min_{q\in J_{\bar\mu}} \frac{\Theta_q(\mu)}{\Theta_q(\bar\mu)}, \qquad \alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}^{\min\Theta}(\mu) =c(\mu;\bar\mu)\,\bar\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}.\]

The minimum must be strictly positive for this formula to certify a positive bound. A reference-inactive term \(\Theta_q(\bar\mu)=0\) has no ratio; its nonnegative contribution at \(\mu\) can simply be dropped when deriving this bound.

Proposition — Validity of the min-Theta lower bound

Under the hypotheses above, whenever \(c(\mu;\bar\mu)>0\),

\[0<\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}^{\min\Theta}(\mu) \leq\alpha(\mu).\]
Proof

For every active term, \(\Theta_q(\mu)\geq c(\mu;\bar\mu)\Theta_q(\bar\mu)\). Multiplying by the nonnegative \(a_q(v,v)\) preserves the inequality. For a reference-inactive term, \(\Theta_q(\mu)a_q(v,v)\geq0\). Sum the terms and use reference coercivity:

\[\begin{aligned} a_\mu(v,v) &=\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\Theta_q(\mu)a_q(v,v)\\ &\geq c(\mu;\bar\mu) \sum_{q=1}^{Q}\Theta_q(\bar\mu)a_q(v,v)\\ &=c(\mu;\bar\mu)a_{\bar\mu}(v,v)\\ &\geq c(\mu;\bar\mu)\bar\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}\|v\|_V^2. \end{aligned}\]

Take the infimum over nonzero \(v\). Positivity follows from \(c>0\) and \(\bar\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}>0\).

If the fixed norm is defined by the reference energy, \(\|v\|_V^2=a_{\bar\mu}(v,v)\), then \(\alpha(\bar\mu)=1\), and the formula reduces to \(\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}^{\min\Theta}=\min_q\Theta_q(\mu)/\Theta_q(\bar\mu)\). In another fixed norm, the factor \(\bar\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}\) must remain; silently omitting it changes the units and can invalidate the certificate.

Example: the two-parameter 1D practical

The 1D practical has \(A(\mu)=\mu K+M\) and \(f(g)=b+g e_n\). The diffusion parameter \(\mu>0\) affects the operator; the right-endpoint flux \(g\) affects only the load. Thus the coercivity constant depends on \(\mu\), not on \(g\). Take \(\Theta_1(\mu)=\mu\), \(\Theta_2(\mu)=1\), and \(\bar\mu=1\). Both component quadratic forms are nonnegative. The practical uses the fixed reference-energy metric \(G=A(1)=K+M\), so \(\alpha(1)=1\). Let \(\lambda_{\min}(K,M)\) and \(\lambda_{\max}(K,M)\) be the extremal generalized eigenvalues of \(K v=\lambda M v\). Then

\[\bar\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}=\alpha(1)=1,\qquad \alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}^{\min\Theta}(\mu)=\min\{\mu,1\}, \qquad \alpha(\mu)= \min_{\lambda\in\{\lambda_{\min},\lambda_{\max}\}} \frac{1+\mu\lambda}{1+\lambda}.\]

The last formula follows by diagonalizing the pair \((K,M)\): the quotient is monotone in \(\lambda\), so its minimum lies at one of the two extremes. Evaluating the min-Theta bound at a new \(\mu\) needs only two scalar coefficients and a minimum. The lower bound is valid but can be pessimistic when \(\mu\) is well above the reference. Dividing a fixed residual norm by a smaller lower bound enlarges the error bound and its effectivity. The practical lets you compare the exact constant and min-Theta directly. Choosing \(G=A(\bar\mu)\) makes the ratio-only min-Theta formula correct without a separate reference-coercivity factor.

When the shortcut cannot be used

Nonnegative coefficients alone are insufficient: the component quadratic forms must also be nonnegative. For example, with \(G=I\), \(A_1=\operatorname{diag}(2,-1)\), \(A_2=\operatorname{diag}(-1,2)\), and \(\Theta(\bar\mu)=(1,1)\), the reference operator is \(I\). At \(\Theta(\mu)=(1.5,1)\), the new operator is \(\operatorname{diag}(2,0.5)\) and has coercivity 0.5. A naive coefficient minimum of 1 would falsely claim a lower bound of 1.

If a coefficient changes sign, a component is indefinite, or the coefficient minimum is zero, the displayed formula does not provide the required positive certificate. Changing the reference or deriving another analytic lower bound may help; for more general affine operators, the successive constraint method (SCM) is a separate advanced approach. For a nonsymmetric operator, coercivity depends on its symmetric part; apply the argument to an affine decomposition of that part with nonnegative component quadratic forms.

What is done offline and online?

Stage Work

Offline

Choose \(\bar\mu\) and the fixed norm; verify positivity of the component forms and coefficients; establish a certified \(\bar\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}\) for the reference full-order problem; store \(\Theta_q(\bar\mu)\).

Online

Evaluate the \(Q\) scalar functions \(\Theta_q(\mu)\), take the ratio minimum, and multiply by \(\bar\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}\). The cost is independent of the full state dimension \(n\).

This makes the stability factor cheap. The residual dual norm is a separate part of the estimator and needs its own offline/online treatment if the entire certificate must be independent of \(n\); see the advanced residual factorization.
