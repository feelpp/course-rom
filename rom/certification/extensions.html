Beyond the symmetric steady case

Purpose: a short bridge from residual bounds to general outputs, nonsymmetric equations and efficient time-dependent prediction. The main example is a two-dimensional thermal finite-element model reduced with POD. Full adjoint analysis and efficient residual-norm factorization can be studied later; the offline/online construction of the prediction is essential here.

Three changes, three questions

Extension What changes? What remains useful?

Non-compliant output

The requested quantity \(s=\ell(u)\) is not the load functional \(f(u)\): for example, an average temperature in a selected region.

Galerkin prediction and the state bound; a dual problem can target this particular output.

Nonsymmetric operator

For example, advection introduces a preferred direction and \(A\ne A^T\).

Projection and affine decomposition; coercive problems retain the basic residual bound.

Time dependence

The state depends on its initial condition and its earlier evolution.

A fixed reduced space and projected operators; training and error assessment must cover trajectories.

These changes are independent: an ordinary symmetric heat equation can have a non-compliant output, and a nonsymmetric problem may still be coercive. Throughout, bounds concern the difference between reduced and full discrete models with the same parameters, forcing and discretization. They do not include mesh error, time-discretization error, parameter mismatch or measurement noise.

A general output: use a dual problem when needed

Keep the full system \(Au_h=f\), the reduced state \(u_r=Za\) and the primal residual \(\rho_p=f-Au_r\). Let \(G\) be the fixed positive-definite state metric, \(\|v\|_V^2=v^TGv\), and \(\|b\|_{V'}^2=b^TG^{-1}b\) for a functional’s coefficient vector. The Riesz construction computes this dual norm by a solve, without explicitly forming an inverse. If \(v^TAv\geq\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}v^TGv\) with a verified \(\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}>0\), the inexpensive conceptual extension is

\[|s_h-s_r|\leq\|\ell\|_{V'}\frac{\|\rho_p\|_{V'}}{\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}}, \qquad s_h=\ell^Tu_h,\quad s_r=\ell^Tu_r.\]

It bounds every bounded linear output, but can be pessimistic: a large state error may barely affect the chosen output. The symmetric compliant identity \(s_h-s_r=\|u_h-u_r\|_A^2\) no longer applies to an arbitrary output.

Dual idea. Solve \(A^Tz=\ell\). The vector \(z\) measures how residuals influence this output, because \(s_h-s_r=z^T\rho_p\). Approximate it in its own reduced space: \(z_d=Wb\), with \(W^TA^TWb=W^T\ell\). Define the dual residual \(\rho_d=\ell-A^Tz_d\) and correct the output:

\[s_r^{\mathrm{corr}}=\ell^Tu_r+z_d^T\rho_p, \qquad |s_h-s_r^{\mathrm{corr}}| \leq\frac{\|\rho_p\|_{V'}\|\rho_d\|_{V'}}{\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}}.\]

The reason fits in one line: \(s_h-s_r^{\mathrm{corr}}=(u_h-u_r)^T\rho_d\); apply the primal state bound and the dual-norm inequality. This product bound becomes small when both approximations improve; it does not promise a better numerical bound for every choice of dual space. Our dual sign convention is \(A^Tz=+\ell\), hence the plus sign in the correction. Using \(W=Z\) makes the correction zero by Galerkin orthogonality, so an independently trained or enriched dual space is usually useful. Different outputs generally need different dual information; a simple state-based bound may suffice when many outputs are requested.

Nonsymmetric does not automatically mean unstable

For a coercive nonsymmetric matrix, Galerkin still solves \(Z^TAZa=Z^Tf\), and

\[\|u_h-u_r\|_V\leq\frac{\|\rho_p\|_{V'}}{\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}}, \qquad \|u_h-u_r\|_V\leq\frac{\gamma}{\alpha} \inf_{v_r\in V_r}\|u_h-v_r\|_V.\]

Here \(\gamma\) is the continuity constant and \(\alpha\) the coercivity constant in the same norm. The proof uses coercivity of the symmetric part \((A+A^T)/2\), not symmetry of the whole operator. Energy-best-approximation and the symmetric compliant-output identity must not be reused unchanged. For example, adding a skew-symmetric transport part to a coercive diffusion operator preserves its quadratic coercivity, while changing its solutions and continuity constant.

If coercivity is lost, the relevant quantity is an inf-sup constant: a lower bound on how strongly the operator acts between the chosen trial and test norms. A verified positive lower bound \(\beta_{\mathrm{LB}}\) gives a state estimate \(\|e\|_V\leq\|\rho_p\|_{V'}/\beta_{\mathrm{LB}}\) in the equal-space finite-dimensional setting used here. However, an invertible full system does not ensure a stable projected system: stabilized or Petrov–Galerkin spaces may be needed. That is a further extension, not a change of denominator that makes every reduced method stable.

Time dependence: evolve the reduced coefficients

Consider a fixed parameter during one trajectory and a fixed reduced basis:

\[M(\mu)\dot u_h+A(\mu)u_h=f(t;\mu),\qquad u_h(0)=u_h^0, \qquad s(t)=\ell^Tu_h(t).\]

Assume \(M\) is symmetric positive definite and constant in time, and \(A\) is coercive (possibly nonsymmetric). Set \(u_r(t)=Za(t)\). Testing gives

\[M_r\dot a+A_ra=f_r(t),\qquad M_r=Z^TMZ,\quad A_r=Z^TAZ,\quad f_r=Z^Tf.\]

Project the initial state with \(M_ra^0=Z^TMu_h^0\); retain the initial projection error in any certificate. Mass orthonormality makes \(M_r=I\) only for that same mass matrix. A POD basis normalized with a reference mass need not be orthonormal for every parameter-dependent mass.

For backward Euler with step \(\Delta t\), both full and reduced models use the same time grid. The reduced step is

\[(M_r+\Delta t A_r)a^k=M_ra^{k-1}+\Delta t f_r(t_k), \qquad s_r^k=\ell_r^Ta^k,\quad\ell_r=Z^T\ell.\]

The term \(M_ra^{k-1}\) carries memory. Time cannot be treated as a collection of unrelated steady solves. For zero forcing, multiplying this equation by \(a^k\) shows that the mass norm cannot increase under these assumptions: backward Euler and coercive Galerkin retain dissipative stability. This is a stability statement, not a guarantee that a small space represents all transients accurately.

The error accumulates over the trajectory

Let \(e^k=u_h^k-Za^k\), \(\|v\|_M^2=v^TMv\), and define the full discrete residual

\[\rho^k=f(t_k)-MZ\frac{a^k-a^{k-1}}{\Delta t}-AZa^k.\]

It includes the time derivative. A steady residual \(f-AZa^k\) would also measure the physical storage term and is not the appropriate error residual. With the same fixed state metric \(G\) as above and a positive coercivity lower bound, a simple discrete energy estimate is

\[\|e^K\|_M^2+ \alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}\sum_{k=1}^K\Delta t\,\|e^k\|_V^2 \leq \|e^0\|_M^2+ \frac{1}{\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}}\sum_{k=1}^K\Delta t\,\|\rho^k\|_{V'}^2 =:D_K^2.\]

To see where it comes from, subtract the two time-stepping equations, test with \(e^k\), use the identity for \(2(e^k-e^{k-1})^TMe^k\), and apply Young’s inequality to the residual term. Summation telescopes the mass energies. The right-hand side includes both the initial error and the entire residual history; the last residual alone cannot certify the current state. Dropping the nonnegative sum on the left gives \(\|e^K\|_M\leq D_K\). For a terminal linear output,

\[|s_h^K-s_r^K|\leq\sqrt{\ell^TM^{-1}\ell}\,D_K.\]

This output uses the mass-dual norm, whereas the residual sum uses the state-dual norm defined by \(G\). They are generally different. For a sharper bound on a final-time output, the dual problem runs backwards from a terminal condition. For a time-integrated output it also has a source term. A consistent transient dual correction combines residuals over time and an initial-condition term; the steady product formula cannot simply be applied to the final residual. Time-varying mass matrices and noncoercive evolution require additional analysis beyond this introductory estimate.

Offline/online: reducing every repeated operation

Suppose the parameter dependence is affine in the RB sense:

\[M(\mu)=\sum_q\theta_q^M(\mu)M_q,\qquad A(\mu)=\sum_q\theta_q^A(\mu)A_q,\qquad f(t;\mu)=\sum_q\theta_q^f(t;\mu)f_q.\]

The scalar coefficient functions may be nonlinear in the parameter; the spatial matrices and vectors are fixed. The same principle covers affine outputs and parameterized initial conditions.

Stage Compute Why it matters

Offline, once per basis

Full trajectories, POD basis; \(Z^TM_qZ\), \(Z^TA_qZ\), \(Z^Tf_q\), \(Z^T\ell\). Project any fixed initial-condition components too.

All operations involving the full state dimension are paid during preparation.

Online, once per parameter

Combine the reduced mass and stiffness terms; project/assemble the initial coefficients from stored components; factor \(M_r+\Delta t A_r\).

For fixed parameters, operators and time step, reuse this factorization throughout the trajectory.

Online, each time step

Combine the reduced load, form the small right-hand side, solve using the stored factors, evaluate outputs.

No full matrix assembly, full vector or full-state reconstruction is needed for scalar prediction.

Only when a field is requested

Reconstruct \(Za^k\).

This costs \(O(nr)\) and must be reported separately in timings.

With modest affine counts, dense reduced algebra costs about \(O((Q_A+Q_M)r^2+r^3)\) per query setup and \(O(r^2+Q_fr)\) per time step, plus output evaluation. This is independent of the full dimension \(n\), but still proportional to the number of time steps. Changing the time step or operators can require a new factorization. An arbitrary new full-dimensional initial state also costs full-dimensional work to project; a strict online budget needs a fixed/affine initial state or already reduced initial data.

General outputs. For a dual basis \(W\), also precompute \(W^TA_q^TW\), \(W^T\ell\), \(W^Tf_q\) and the cross matrices \(W^TA_qZ\). Then the corrected steady output is evaluated entirely with small arrays:

\[s_r^{\mathrm{corr}}=\ell_r^Ta+ b^T\left(\sum_q\theta_q^f W^Tf_q- \sum_q\theta_q^A(W^TA_qZ)a\right).\]

Optional certification extension. The transient residual is a combination of fixed columns \(f_q\), \(A_qZ\) and \(M_qZ\). Offline Riesz representatives and a stable residual factor allow its dual norm to be evaluated from small coefficients online; a dual certificate needs the corresponding transposed-operator columns too. This extends the existing residual-factor construction. Without that extra preparation, prediction can be fast while certification still costs full-dimensional work. Non-affine or nonlinear terms require another approximation, such as EIM/DEIM; its error must then enter the error assessment.

Course application: a transient 2D thermal-fin model

Use temperature relative to a fixed ambient value on a fixed two-dimensional fin domain. The PDE is heat diffusion with heat storage, \(c\,\partial_t T-\nabla\cdot(\kappa\nabla T)=0\), with imposed heat input at the root and Robin cooling on the exposed boundary. The supplied model is nondimensional; fixed heat capacity is represented by the mass matrix. The finite-element mass matrix represents heat storage; five conduction matrices describe the material subregions, and a Robin boundary matrix describes heat loss:

\[M\dot u+A(\mu)u=f g(t),\qquad A(\mu)=\sum_{q=0}^4\kappa_q A_q+\mathrm{Bi}\,A_5.\]

Positive conductivities and a positive Robin coefficient give a coercive diffusion problem when the boundary removes the constant mode. A mean temperature, a regional average or a sensor prediction is a general output; it need not equal the imposed heat-input functional. The supplied thermal-fin data and model already provide a 2D mesh, these operators and a mass matrix for 1333 degrees of freedom. The first experiment can keep all conductivities at one, vary \(\mathrm{Bi}\), use a fixed zero initial state, and prescribe heating followed by cooling.

POD must learn trajectories. Concatenate snapshots \(u_h(t_k;\mu_j)\) from several training parameters and times; include heating, cooling and early transients. Use the fixed mass matrix as the state metric and weights combining parameter sampling with time quadrature. In particular, nonuniform sampling in time should not accidentally give densely sampled intervals excessive importance. The weighted correlation construction is

\[C=D^{1/2}S^TMSD^{1/2},\qquad Cv_i=\lambda_i v_i,\qquad z_i=\frac{SD^{1/2}v_i}{\sqrt{\lambda_i}}.\]

Here \(D\) contains positive snapshot weights, and only positive, numerically resolved eigenvalues are retained. It gives \(Z^TMZ=I\) and the optimal weighted training projection error \(\sum_{i>r}\lambda_i\). This tail is not a bound on a newly integrated reduced trajectory: test that trajectory at held-out parameters and forcing histories.

For this positive affine thermal operator, one can even avoid a complicated stability-estimation procedure. Choose the fixed certification metric \(G=\sum_{q=0}^4 A_q+\mathrm{Bi}_{\mathrm{ref}}A_5\), assumed positive definite, with \(\mathrm{Bi}_{\mathrm{ref}}>0\). Because the individual diffusion and Robin matrices are positive semidefinite,

\[\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}(\mu)= \min\left\{\kappa_0,\ldots,\kappa_4, \frac{\mathrm{Bi}}{\mathrm{Bi}_{\mathrm{ref}}}\right\} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad A(\mu)\succeq\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}(\mu)G.\]

The mass metric for POD and the stiffness-based metric for residual certification have distinct roles. This lower bound is positive on the stated parameter range; it does not cover a pure-Neumann limit with zero heat loss.

A prediction-focused practical can follow this sequence:

  1. Compute full backward-Euler trajectories for a small training sample.

  2. Build a mass-weighted POD basis and inspect its eigenvalue tail.

  3. Precompute affine reduced operators and output vectors.

  4. At a held-out parameter, factor the small step matrix once and integrate the reduced coefficients.

  5. Compare mass-norm trajectory error, final/mean temperature error and heating/cooling curves against the full discrete model.

  6. Measure offline cost, online setup and stepping separately; compare the same requested outputs and exclude plotting from the prediction timing.

  7. As an extension, accumulate the transient residual bound and compare it with the observed error. Assimilation can then use this ROM as its forecast model.

The existing thermal-fin practical supplies a runnable starting point, already coupled to EnKF. The sequence above isolates reduced prediction before introducing the observation update; it prepares the more detailed parabolic problems without requiring a new assimilation method.

Results to retain

Case Main result Condition or limitation

General steady output

State-based output bound; optional primal–dual product bound for a corrected output.

Bounded linear output and verified stability; no general positive sign or squared-energy identity.

Nonsymmetric, coercive

Residual state bound and Céa estimate remain valid.

Use coercivity, not symmetric energy optimality. Noncoercive reduction needs a stability treatment of its own.

Parabolic, backward Euler

Stable reduced evolution; accumulated residual bound on final mass-norm error and trajectory energy.

Fixed positive mass, coercive operator, consistent time grids; include initial error.

Trajectory POD

Optimal weighted projection of the training snapshots.

Does not by itself certify new trajectories, inputs or outputs.

Affine online prediction

Small assembly and factorization, followed by small time steps and output evaluations.

Fixed basis, inexpensive coefficient evaluation, modest affine counts; full reconstruction and certification have separate costs.
