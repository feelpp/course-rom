A posteriori error estimation for coercive reduced models

The practical question is whether a cheap reduced prediction is accurate enough at a new parameter, without solving the full model again. A residual becomes a bound only after combining it with stability information in consistent norms. Prerequisites: Galerkin reduction and Hilbert spaces, dual norms and Riesz representation.

Core course route: understand the residual functional, compute its norm with a Riesz solve, derive the bound and interpret effectivity. The final offline residual factorization is optional advanced reading; it is not required for the session 3 checkpoint. Continue with the extension overview for general outputs, nonsymmetric operators and parabolic problems, including efficient time stepping and the 2D thermal/POD application.

Residual and error equation

For fixed \(\mu\), let \(e=u_h-u_r\) and define the residual functional

\[R_\mu(v)=f_\mu(v)-a_\mu(u_r,v),\qquad a_\mu(e,v)=R_\mu(v)\quad\text{for all }v\in V_h.\]

From the residual functional to a computable norm

The residual assigns a scalar imbalance to each test direction. Its norm measures the largest imbalance per unit test norm:

\[\|R_\mu\|_{V_h'}=\sup_{v\ne0}\frac{|R_\mu(v)|}{\|v\|_V}.\]

Rather than searching over infinitely many vectors, use the Riesz representative \(z_\mu\in V_h\), defined by

\[(z_\mu,v)_V=R_\mu(v)\quad\text{for every }v\in V_h.\]

Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the quotient by \(\|z_\mu\|_V\), and testing in the direction of this representative attains the bound. Thus the dual norm is exactly its state norm; see the Riesz theorem and norm proof. In a discrete basis, the computation becomes

\[\boldsymbol\rho=\mathbf f-AZ\mathbf a,\qquad G\mathbf z_\mu=\boldsymbol\rho,\qquad \|R_\mu\|_{V_h'}=\sqrt{\boldsymbol\rho^T\mathbf z_\mu} =\sqrt{\mathbf z_\mu^TG\mathbf z_\mu}.\]

The matrix \(G\) represents the chosen inner product; it is not generally \(A(\mu)\). The error solves \(A(\mu)\mathbf e=\boldsymbol\rho\), whereas the representative solves the metric system above. For \(G=I\), the representative equals the residual vector and its norm is Euclidean. For a general metric, the two-dimensional worked example shows why this shortcut fails.

# Given a reduced prediction ur, a metric G, and a verified positive lower bound:
rho = f - A @ ur
z_residual = np.linalg.solve(G, rho)
residual_dual_norm = np.sqrt(rho @ z_residual)
delta = residual_dual_norm / alpha_lb

This direct implementation is sufficient to understand and test the estimator in class. It still performs full-dimensional operations. A fixed factorization of \(G\) can be reused, but even its triangular solves depend on \(n\). Avoid explicitly forming an inverse. The affine prediction algorithm is already cheap independently of how its certificate is evaluated.

A Galerkin solution has \(Z^T\boldsymbol\rho=0\) up to solver error. This tests only reduced directions. The full residual functional, its representative and the state error can all remain nonzero.

Theorem — Residual bound in a fixed state norm

Assume \(a_\mu(v,v)\geq\alpha(\mu)\|v\|_V^2\) for all \(v\in V_h\), and that a verified lower bound satisfies \(0<\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}(\mu)\leq\alpha(\mu)\). Then

\[\|u_h-u_r\|_V\leq\frac{\|R_\mu\|_{V_h'}}{\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}(\mu)}=:\Delta_r(\mu).\]
Proof

Test the error equation with \(e\). Coercivity, the error equation, and then the dual-norm inequality give, in that order,

\[\alpha\|e\|_V^2\leq a_\mu(e,e)=R_\mu(e) \leq |R_\mu(e)|\leq\|R_\mu\|_{V_h'}\|e\|_V.\]

If \(e=0\), the claim is immediate. Otherwise divide by \(\alpha\|e\|_V\) and replace \(\alpha\) with its positive lower bound.

An estimated stability constant that might exceed the true value does not supply this guarantee. For a linear output \(s=\ell(u)\), \(|s_h-s_r|\leq\|\ell\|_{V_h'}\Delta_r\). This bound concerns reduction error relative to the discrete full-order solution. It does not certify model discrepancy or observational noise.

Effectivity and a computable example

A bound answers “how large could the error be?” Its effectivity answers “how pessimistic is that bound compared with the actual error?” For a nonzero error, define

\[\eta_r(\mu)=\frac{\Delta_r(\mu)}{\|u_h(\mu)-u_r(\mu)\|_V}.\]

  • \(\eta_r=1\): the bound is sharp for this prediction.

  • \(\eta_r>1\): the bound overestimates the error by this factor; ten means ten times the actual error.

  • \(\eta_r<1\): the claimed bound fails for this case, apart from a declared numerical tolerance. Check assumptions, norms, stability lower bounds and implementation.

Effectivity is an evaluation diagnostic: computing its denominator requires the full-order reference solution. Evaluating the certificate itself does not require that solution. A large effectivity does not imply a large state error: an error of \(10^{-6}\) and a bound of \(10^{-4}\) give effectivity 100, while both absolute quantities may be acceptable. Conversely, a sharp bound can correctly report an unacceptably large error.

Why effectivity can exceed one

Continuity gives the other side of the residual/error comparison. For any test direction,

\[|R_\mu(v)|=|a_\mu(e,v)|\leq\gamma(\mu)\|e\|_V\|v\|_V.\]

Taking a supremum and combining with coercivity yields

\[\alpha(\mu)\|e\|_V\leq\|R_\mu\|_{V_h'}\leq\gamma(\mu)\|e\|_V, \qquad 1\leq\eta_r(\mu)\leq\frac{\gamma(\mu)}{\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}(\mu)}.\]

The lower effectivity bound uses the validity of \(\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}\). A loose lower bound adds pessimism, but even the exact coercivity constant need not give effectivity one: it describes the least stable direction, while this error may point elsewhere. If a known upper bound \(\gamma_{\mathrm{UB}}\geq\gamma\) is available, the residual also supplies a lower error bound, as summarized below.

Worked example: sharpness depends on the error direction

Let \(A=\operatorname{diag}(1,4)\) and \(G=I\), so the exact coercivity and continuity constants are 1 and 4. For a unit error in the first coordinate, the residual norm is 1. For a unit error in the second coordinate, it is 4. These cases can arise from Galerkin approximation using the other coordinate as the reduced space.

Reaction–diffusion practical

For \(A(\mu)=\mu K+I\), \(K=h^{-2}\operatorname{tridiag}(-1,2,-1)\), and the Euclidean state metric \(G=I\),

\[\alpha(\mu)=1+\mu\lambda_{\min}(K),\qquad \lambda_{\min}(K)=\frac{4}{h^2}\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2(n+1)}\right).\]

For \(\mu>0\), both the exact formula and the weaker value \(\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}=1\) are valid lower bounds. With the physical grid norm \(G=hI\), the bilinear form \(\mathbf v^TA\mathbf w\) has coercivity constant \(\lambda_{\min}(A)/h\) and residual dual norm \(\|\boldsymbol\rho\|_2/\sqrt h\). Their ratio is \(\sqrt h\|\boldsymbol\rho\|_2/\lambda_{\min}(A)\), consistent with the scaled state norm. Changing a norm requires changing both quantities consistently.

Summary of the main error bounds

Write \(e=u_h-u_r\), \(d_r=\inf_{v_r\in V_r}\Vert u_h-v_r\Vert _V\) and \(r_D=\Vert R_\mu\Vert _{V_h'}\). All norms and stability constants in a row must refer to the same state space and inner product. The table concerns the discrete full-order model. For the POD row, define the weighted training projection error by

\[E_{\mathrm{POD}}^2=\sum_jw_j\|u_j-P_Gu_j\|_G^2.\]
Result Bound or identity Assumptions and interpretation

Galerkin quasi-optimality (Céa)

\(\Vert e\Vert _V\leq(\gamma/\alpha)d_r\)

Continuous, coercive bilinear form; conforming Galerkin space. Relates solve error to the best available approximation; not a computable certificate unless \(d_r\) is known.

Symmetric Galerkin improvement

\(\Vert e\Vert _V\leq\sqrt{\gamma/\alpha}\,d_r\)

Also symmetry. In the energy norm, the Galerkin solution is exactly the best approximation. See the proof.

Residual upper bound

\(\Vert e\Vert _V\leq r_D/\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}=\Delta_r\)

Verified \(0<\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}\leq\alpha\). Computable without the full solution.

Residual lower bound

\(r_D/\gamma_{\mathrm{UB}}\leq\Vert e\Vert _V\)

Verified continuity upper bound \(\gamma_{\mathrm{UB}}\geq\gamma\). Complements the preceding upper bound.

Effectivity

\(1\leq\eta_r\leq\gamma/\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}\)

Nonzero error; valid residual bound. Evaluating the actual ratio requires a reference solution.

Linear output

\(\lvert s_h-s_r\rvert\leq\Vert \ell\Vert _{V_h'}\Delta_r\)

Bounded linear output. Its dual norm is computed with the same Riesz map.

Compliant output (symmetric case)

\(s_h-s_r=\Vert e\Vert _{a_\mu}^2\leq r_D^2/\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}\)

Output equals the load functional; conforming Galerkin and symmetry. Nonnegative, quadratic error. See the identity and proof.

POD training identity

\(E_{\mathrm{POD}}^2=\sum_{i>r}\sigma_i^2\)

Weighted SVD and declared snapshot metric/weights. Exact optimal average projection error on training data; not a parameterwise solve certificate. See POD.

For the output row, apply the dual-norm inequality to \(\ell(e)\) and then the state-error bound. For the first row, Galerkin orthogonality gives, for every \(v_r\in V_r\),

\[\alpha\|e\|_V^2\leq a_\mu(e,e) =a_\mu(e,u_h-v_r) \leq\gamma\|e\|_V\|u_h-v_r\|_V.\]

Divide for nonzero error and take the infimum. Symmetry improves this argument through energy optimality. These guarantees do not include sensor noise, parameter mismatch or discretization error relative to the continuous PDE.

Advanced reading — Offline/online evaluation of the bound

Optional; outside the required class derivation and homework. For prediction, affine decomposition simply permits projection of each fixed operator once. For a residual norm, an additional construction is needed because the residual contains both loads and operator actions on the basis. This section explains that construction for students who want a certificate whose evaluation also avoids the full dimension. Throughout, the state metric is fixed and parameter independent.

Collect the residual in fixed columns

For the running affine model,

\[\boldsymbol\rho=\mathbf f-\mu KZ\mathbf a-Z\mathbf a=B\mathbf c, \quad B=[\mathbf f\ KZ\ Z],\quad \mathbf c=(1,-\mu\mathbf a^T,-\mathbf a^T)^T.\]

Here \(B\) has \(n\) rows and \(p=1+2r\) columns. The large columns depend on the basis, while the small vector \(\mathbf c\) depends on the query and its reduced coefficients. For general affine operators and loads, collect the load vectors followed by each block \(A_qZ\). The corresponding coefficient blocks are the load weights and \(-\theta_q(\mu)\mathbf a\); there are \(p=Q_f+Q_A r\) columns.

Apply the Riesz map offline

Solve \(GD=B\) for all the fixed columns and store \(C=B^TD\). Then

\[\|R_\mu\|_{V_h'}^2=\mathbf c^TC\mathbf c.\]

This is the Gram matrix of the column functionals in the dual inner product. It uses \(O(p^2)\) online arithmetic and storage, independent of \(n\) when \(p\) is modest. Near a vanishing residual, cancellation among large terms can make the computed square inaccurate or slightly negative. A negative value is a numerical diagnostic, not a negative norm.

A weighted residual factor

With \(G=L^TL\), solve for the weighted residual columns and compute a thin QR factorization:

\[L^TY=B,\qquad Y=Q_R T,\qquad Q_R^TQ_R=I, \qquad \|R_\mu\|_{V_h'}=\|T\mathbf c\|_2.\]

The identity follows from the dual-coordinate transformation, then the isometry of \(Q_R\). For \(p\leq n\), \(T\) is \(p\times p\); dependent columns are allowed, with zero diagonal entries in exact arithmetic. For \(p>n\), a reduced QR gives a rectangular \(T\); the same norm identity holds, but this ceases to be a useful small representation. This form avoids the final squared Gram expression, although cancellation and roundoff can still affect extremely small residuals.

# Offline, after the reduced basis is fixed:
L = np.linalg.cholesky(G).T
B = np.column_stack([f, K @ Z, Z])
Y = np.linalg.solve(L.T, B)
_, T = np.linalg.qr(Y, mode="reduced")

# At a query, after solving the reduced prediction for a:
c = np.r_[1., -mu * a, -a]
delta = np.linalg.norm(T @ c) / alpha_lb

The estimator still requires a cheaply evaluable, verified positive stability lower bound. For the running model this is supplied analytically; obtaining one for a general PDE is a separate advanced topic. Rebuild or update the residual factor when greedy enrichment changes the basis. The core greedy selection logic only needs a callable valid bound; it does not require students to implement this factorization.
