Notation and mathematical conventions

These conventions connect the reduction and assimilation parts of the library. Historical slides sometimes use different symbols; each converted chapter should state the correspondence. The M2 course selects a short route through these topics.

States, spaces and parameters

Symbol Meaning

\(\mu\in\mathcal P\)

Physical or geometric parameter; a parameter vector when necessary.

\(V_h\), \(n=\dim V_h\)

Discrete full-order state space and its dimension.

\(u_h(\mu)\), \(\mathbf u(\mu)\in\mathbb R^n\)

Discrete state and its coefficient vector in a specified full-order basis.

\(V_r\subset V_h\), \(r\ll n\)

Reduced trial space and its dimension.

\(Z\in\mathbb R^{n\times r}\)

Full-column-rank reduced basis matrix.

\(\mathbf a(\mu)\in\mathbb R^r\)

Reduced coefficients; reconstructed state \(\mathbf u_r=Z\mathbf a\).

\(s(\mu)=\ell(u_h(\mu))\)

Quantity of interest. A scalar output is distinct from the full state and from the observation vector.

Use \(\mathbf a\) for reduced coefficients and \(a_\mu(\cdot,\cdot)\) for a bilinear form; make the distinction explicit. A reduced model approximates the discrete full-order model. Its error relative to the physical system also involves discretization error and model discrepancy.

Inner products, projections and POD

Specify a symmetric positive definite Gram matrix \(G\) before reporting a state norm:

\[\langle\mathbf v,\mathbf w\rangle_G=\mathbf v^T G\mathbf w, \qquad \|\mathbf v\|_G=\sqrt{\mathbf v^TG\mathbf v}.\]

A mass matrix may represent an \(L^2\) inner product; another Gram matrix may represent an energy or \(H^1\) inner product. The Euclidean norm corresponds to \(G=I\). Do not substitute it for a physical norm without stating the change. For a uniform one-dimensional grid, a discrete integral often contains a factor \(h\); use the same convention in errors, Riesz representatives and sensor stability calculations.

Proposition — Projection in a specified metric

If \(Z\) has full column rank and \(G\) is symmetric positive definite, the best approximation to \(\mathbf u\) in the range of \(Z\), measured in \(\|\cdot\|_G\), is

\[P_G\mathbf u=Z(Z^TGZ)^{-1}Z^TG\mathbf u.\]

For a \(G\)-orthonormal basis, \(Z^TGZ=I_r\), this becomes \(P_G\mathbf u=ZZ^TG\mathbf u\).

Proof

Minimizing \((\mathbf u-Z\mathbf a)^TG(\mathbf u-Z\mathbf a)\) gives the normal equations \(Z^TGZ\mathbf a=Z^TG\mathbf u\). The matrix is positive definite because \(Z\) has full column rank. The unique solution gives the stated projection.

For POD, record the snapshot parameters, snapshot weights, state metric, centering choice and rank criterion. An unweighted SVD of the snapshot matrix minimizes Euclidean snapshot error; a weighted POD must account for the chosen state metric and sampling weights. Projection error on training snapshots is different from reduced-solve error on unseen parameters.

Residuals and certification

For a discrete linear system \(A(\mu)\mathbf u=\mathbf f(\mu)\), define the residual vector \(\mathbf r=\mathbf f-A\mathbf u_r\). If it represents a functional acting as \(\mathbf r^T\mathbf v\), its dual norm associated with \(G\) is

\[\|r\|_{V_h'}=\sqrt{\mathbf r^TG^{-1}\mathbf r}.\]

Compute this with a solve or a factored reduced expression, not an explicit matrix inverse. A residual norm alone is not an error bound: state the stability assumptions and any certified lower bound used. Write \(\Delta_r\) for a verified upper bound on a stated error norm, and define effectivity as \(\Delta_r/\|u_h-u_r\|_V\) when the denominator is nonzero. Identify whether the bound concerns the discrete model, continuous PDE or an output.

Observations and sequential assimilation

\[\mathbf y_k=H_k\mathbf u_k+\boldsymbol\varepsilon_k, \qquad \operatorname{Cov}(\boldsymbol\varepsilon_k)=R_k.\]

Here \(m\) is the number of observations, \(H_k\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}\) is the linear observation operator, and \(R_k\) is measurement-noise covariance. State explicitly when observations are nonlinear or sensors are continuous functionals. Use \(Q_k\) for process-noise covariance and \(P_k^f,P_k^a\) for forecast and analysis state-error covariances. The superscripts \(f\) and \(a\) mean forecast and analysis; \(k\) indexes time, while \(N_e\) is ensemble size.

GEIM and PBDW chapters must specify both the background-space norm and the sensor functionals, including their normalization. KF/EnKF chapters must specify forecast dynamics, observation operator, covariance assumptions and ensemble initialization. Synthetic truth is available for evaluation in a teaching experiment; an assimilation method must not use that hidden state as an input.
