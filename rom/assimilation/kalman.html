Kalman filtering: forecasts and sequential observations

Static reconstruction uses one observation vector. A filter propagates information through a dynamical model and updates it when new observations arrive. Time index and prior/posterior superscripts must remain distinct.

Linear Gaussian model

\[x_k=F_kx_{k-1}+b_k+w_k,\qquad y_k=H_kx_k+v_k, \qquad w_k\sim\mathcal N(0,Q_k),\quad v_k\sim\mathcal N(0,R_k).\]

Assume the initial state is Gaussian, noises are temporally independent, and process, observation and initial errors are mutually independent. The covariances describe errors, not the temperature or other state’s absolute magnitude. Under these assumptions the Kalman filter computes the conditional mean and covariance exactly. With misspecified covariances its reported uncertainty need not be calibrated.

Forecast

\[m_k^-=F_km_{k-1}^++b_k,\qquad P_k^-=F_kP_{k-1}^+F_k^T+Q_k.\]

The additive input is assumed known. Unknown forcing or model discrepancy needs its own modeling choice; a small reduced-space truncation error does not justify setting process uncertainty to zero.

Analysis

\[\begin{aligned} d_k&=y_k-H_km_k^-, & S_k&=H_kP_k^-H_k^T+R_k,\\ K_k&=P_k^-H_k^TS_k^{-1}, & m_k^+&=m_k^-+K_kd_k. \end{aligned}\]

Use a solve with the innovation covariance. A numerically robust covariance update is the Joseph form:

\[P_k^+=(I-K_kH_k)P_k^-(I-K_kH_k)^T+K_kR_kK_k^T.\]
Why this gain?

For an update using a matrix \(K\), substitute the observation equation into the analysis error. Independence gives the Joseph covariance expression. Completing its quadratic form in \(K\) shows that the stated gain minimizes every directional analysis variance. Equivalently, conditioning the joint Gaussian forecast/observation vector gives these mean and covariance formulas.

Diagnostics

Track forecast-only and assimilated errors on the same observations and declared synthetic truth. Innovations should have approximately zero mean and the predicted covariance when the model is calibrated. For \(p\) independent observed components, the normalized innovation squared has a chi-square distribution with \(p\) degrees of freedom under the exact assumptions. One trajectory or one value outside a confidence interval is not a calibration verdict. A marginal 95% interval is neither a simultaneous band for every time nor a guarantee under model mismatch.

In the practical, compare assumed and actual measurement variance. For nonlinear forecasts and sample covariance approximations, continue to EnKF.

Original reference

R. E. Kalman, A New Approach to Linear Filtering and Prediction Problems (1960).
