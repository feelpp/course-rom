Non-affinity and empirical interpolation

An affine decomposition separates parameter dependence from spatial operators:

\[A(\mu)=\sum_{q=1}^{Q_a}\Theta_q^a(\mu)A_q, \qquad f(\mu)=\sum_{q=1}^{Q_f}\Theta_q^f(\mu)f_q.\]

The worked affine prediction example shows exactly which arrays are stored and used at a query. For small fixed numbers of terms, projected matrices and vectors are assembled offline. A moving Gaussian source or a nonlinear constitutive law generally lacks such a small exact decomposition. Evaluating the entire source or nonlinear residual online can then dominate the reduced solve. Empirical interpolation constructs a separable approximation whose coefficients are determined from selected evaluations.

Connection with POD-greedy

The temperature basis and the basis of a non-affine source play different roles. POD-greedy selects a parameter trajectory using a prediction indicator, then applies mass-POD to its projection defect. EIM instead uses an interpolation defect of the source to build both source basis functions and sampling points. DEIM first fixes a POD basis of source snapshots, then selects points for that basis. These are not the same residual or the same greedy criterion.

EIM: basis functions, generating functions and magic points

We retain the notation of the original non-affine slides. The function family is \(\mathcal G=\{g(\cdot;\mu):\mu\in D_\mu\}\). EIM constructs basis functions \(\rho_j\), an approximation space \(X_M\), and magic points \(T_M=\{t^1,\ldots,t^M\}\):

\[g_M(x;\mu)=I_M^x g(x;\mu)=\sum_{j=1}^M\gamma_j(\mu)\rho_j(x), \qquad X_M=\operatorname{span}\{\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_M\}.\]

The coefficients enforce interpolation at the magic points:

\[\mathbb B_M\boldsymbol\gamma(\mu)=\mathbf g_M(\mu),\qquad (\mathbb B_M)_{ij}=\rho_j(t^i),\qquad (\mathbf g_M(\mu))_i=g(t^i;\mu).\]

Here \(t^i\) is a spatial interpolation point, not a time instant. The scalar-valued function \(g_M(x;\mu)\) and the vector \(\mathbf g_M(\mu)\) of exact sampled values are different objects. At an intermediate iteration we use \(m\) instead of the final size \(M\).

Select the generating function, then the magic point

On a finite training set \(D_{\mu,\mathrm{train}}\), choose

\[\mu^{m+1}_{\mathrm{EIM}}\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\mu\in D_{\mu,\mathrm{train}}} \|g(\cdot;\mu)-I_m^x g(\cdot;\mu)\|_\infty, \qquad \xi_{m+1}(x)=g(x;\mu^{m+1}_{\mathrm{EIM}}).\]

The chosen snapshot is the generating function \(\xi_{m+1}\). Its interpolation residual determines the new magic point and basis function:

\[r_{m+1}=\xi_{m+1}-I_m^x\xi_{m+1},\qquad t^{m+1}\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{x\text{ on grid}}|r_{m+1}(x)|, \qquad \rho_{m+1}(x)=\frac{r_{m+1}(x)}{r_{m+1}(t^{m+1})}.\]

Initialize with \(I_0^x=0\). Normalize by the signed nonzero value, preserving the previous basis functions and magic points. The sets of selected parameters \(S_m=\{\mu^1_{\mathrm{EIM}},\ldots,\mu^m_{\mathrm{EIM}}\}\) and spatial points \(T_m\) have different roles. The residual vanishes at previous magic points, so \(\mathbb B_m\) is lower triangular with unit diagonal in exact arithmetic. This does not imply good conditioning. The spaces are hierarchical, \(X_m\subset X_{m+1\)], and \(I_m^x v=v\) for every \(v\in X_m\). Stop at the requested maximum training residual or at the dimension budget; these are different stopping reasons.

For the notebook, rho stores the sampled basis functions in columns, points contains grid indices, and B_eim = rho[points, :] represents \(\mathbb B_m\). source evaluates \(g\); the snapshot matrix S is not the selected parameter set \(S_m\). On a grid of \(N_q\) spatial evaluation points, the full-grid basis array is denoted \(\mathcal R_m\in\mathbb R^{N_q\times m}\), so the discrete interpolant is \(I_m^x g=\mathcal R_m\mathbb B_m^{-1}P_m^T\mathbf g\). The columns of \(P_m\) are coordinate vectors selecting the magic points. Use a linear solve in code.

Error relative to best approximation

Define the discrete Lebesgue constant \(\Lambda_m=\|I_m^x\|_{\infty\to\infty}\), as in the original slides. For a bounded interpolation operator that is the identity on its range:

\[\|g-I_m^x g\|_\infty\leq (1+\Lambda_m) \inf_{v\in X_m}\|g-v\|_\infty.\]

For any such \(v\), write the error as \((I-I_m^x)(g-v)\) and use the operator norm. For a discrete grid, this constant can be computed. Set \(\mathbb B_m=P_m^T\mathcal R_m\) and obtain \(D=\mathcal R_m\mathbb B_m^{-1}\) by a solve with \(\mathbb B_m^T\). Because \(P_m^T\) only selects distinct coordinates,

\[\|I_m^x\|_{\infty\to\infty}=\max_i\sum_{j=1}^m|D_{ij}|.\]

This is the largest absolute row sum, often called the discrete Lebesgue constant: the triangle inequality proves the upper bound, and choosing signs to match a maximizing row attains it. It measures the worst amplification of a field perturbation, beyond the best-approximation error. The discrete norm concerns this grid; a continuum claim needs control between grid points.

Algorithm — Discrete EIM construction

Collect the training snapshots as columns of \(F\), with parameters \(\mu_j\). A joint maximum of absolute residual entries is equivalent to first selecting the generating function and then its magic point. The algorithm below uses sampled basis columns \(\mathcal R\) and a list of row indices \(p\), with \(t^i=x_{p_i}\).

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{EIM: generating functions and magic points on a finite grid}
\begin{algorithmic}
\INPUT Snapshot matrix $F$; training parameters $\mu_j$; tolerance $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{EIM}}$; budget $M_{\max}$
\STATE $\mathcal R\leftarrow[\,]$; $p\leftarrow[\,]$; $S_0\leftarrow\varnothing$; $m\leftarrow0$
\WHILE{true}
  \IF{$m=0$}
    \STATE $E\leftarrow F$
  \ELSE
    \STATE $\mathbb B_m\leftarrow\mathcal R[p,:]$; solve $\mathbb B_m C=F[p,:]$
    \STATE $E\leftarrow F-\mathcal R C$
  \ENDIF
  \STATE $(i_*,j_*)\leftarrow\operatorname{argmax}_{i,j}|E_{ij}|$
  \IF{$|E_{i_*j_*}|\leq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{EIM}}$}
    \RETURN Basis, magic points and attained training residual (tolerance met)
  \ENDIF
  \IF{$m=M_{\max}$}
    \RETURN Basis, magic points and attained training residual (budget reached)
  \ENDIF
  \STATE $\mu^{m+1}_{\mathrm{EIM}}\leftarrow\mu_{j_*}$; $\xi_{m+1}\leftarrow F_{:,j_*}$
  \STATE $r_{m+1}\leftarrow E_{:,j_*}$; $t^{m+1}\leftarrow x_{i_*}$
  \STATE $\rho_{m+1}\leftarrow r_{m+1}/r_{m+1}(t^{m+1})$
  \STATE Append $\rho_{m+1}$ to $\mathcal R$ and $i_*$ to $p$
  \STATE $S_{m+1}\leftarrow S_m\cup\{\mu^{m+1}_{\mathrm{EIM}}\}$
  \STATE $m\leftarrow m+1$
\ENDWHILE
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Online, evaluate \(g(t^i;\mu)\), solve \(\mathbb B_m\boldsymbol\gamma(\mu)=\mathbf g_m(\mu)\), and use the coefficients in the precomputed reduced expression. Forming the full interpolated field is additional full-dimensional work. For example, if \(\ell(v;\mu)=\int_\Omega g(x;\mu)v(x)\,dx\), then

\[\ell_M(v;\mu)=\sum_{j=1}^M\gamma_j(\mu)\ell_j(v),\qquad \ell_j(v)=\int_\Omega\rho_j(x)v(x)\,dx.\]

Thus EIM recovers an approximate affine expansion with parameter-independent forms, exactly as in the original slides. The maximum over the training grid is a computable diagnostic, not a guarantee over every parameter in \(D_\mu\) or every spatial point between grid nodes.

Coefficient approximation and PDE error

Interpolating a PDE coefficient creates an additional consistency error, separate from Galerkin truncation. For a right-hand-side approximation with a fixed coercive operator, the full solution perturbation satisfies:

\[\|u^{\mathcal N}-\widetilde u^{\mathcal N}\|_X\leq \frac{\|g-\widetilde f\|_{(X^{\mathcal N})'}}{\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}}.\]

Subtract the two equations with the same operator to obtain \(a_\mu(u^{\mathcal N}-\widetilde u^{\mathcal N},v)=(f-\widetilde f)(v)\). The coercivity proof applies with the forcing discrepancy as its right-hand-side functional. Compute that functional’s norm with the same Riesz map; a Euclidean source-vector error is not automatically a dual norm for another metric.

For an interpolated operator, positivity/coercivity can be lost. Check stability of the approximated operator and include its consistency residual; the certificate for the original operator is not automatically unchanged. The course practical starts with a non-affine source to isolate interpolation from this additional difficulty.
