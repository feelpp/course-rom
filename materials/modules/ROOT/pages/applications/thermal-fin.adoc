= Thermal-fin reduced prediction and assimilation
:stem: latexmath

The final two practicals use the course's supplied finite-element thermal-fin matrices.
All files needed by students are published with the notebook; no mesh generator or private source collection is needed.
These are nondimensional teaching experiments, not a calibrated industrial twin.

== Model and data

The spatial operators describe conduction in five subregions and Robin heat loss on the exposed boundary.
We set all five conductivities to one and vary the Biot number multiplying the boundary matrix.
A supplied mass matrix enables a transient extension of the original spatial model:

[stem]
++++
M\dot u+A(\mathrm{Bi})u=f g(t),\qquad
A(\mathrm{Bi})=\sum_{q=0}^{4}A_q+\mathrm{Bi}\,A_5.
++++

The coarse grid has 1333 degrees of freedom.
The xref:course-rom::attachment$data/thermal-fin-coarse.npz[compact coarse data] are extracted from the published xref:course-rom::attachment$homework-2024-data.zip[original MATLAB data].
Sparse matrices retain their assembled values. Coordinates and triangles support interpretation and plots.
The prescribed time-dependent input, time grid, training parameters, synthetic noise and assimilation experiment are defined in the new practical.

== Mass-weighted POD

For a snapshot matrix, use the small correlation eigenproblem:

[stem]
++++
S^TMS\,v_j=\lambda_jv_j,\qquad
z_j=\frac{Sv_j}{\sqrt{\lambda_j}},\qquad Z^TMZ=I.
++++

Only positive, numerically resolved eigenvalues may be retained.
Uniform snapshot weights multiply the correlation by a constant and leave the span unchanged; they change the reported average-error normalization.
This is the mass-metric counterpart of the PCA/POD connection from xref:reduction/pod.adoc[session 2].
Avoid constructing a dense square root of the full mass matrix when the snapshot correlation is small.

== Experimental separation

Train at declared Biot numbers and hold a different one out for evaluation.
Use the same deliberately imperfect forecast parameter for full and reduced forecast-only baselines.
Their difference measures reduction of that forecast model; their difference from synthetic truth also contains parameter mismatch.
Use observations to update reduced coefficients, with a specified process covariance.
Truth must never initialize the filter from an unknown state or choose its online parameters.

Record mass-norm state error, mean-temperature error, sensor innovations and online cost.
Mean temperature uses the mass-weighted integral of the reconstructed finite-element field.
State estimates used for diagnostics may be reconstructed offline; scalar prediction and sensor updates use precomputed reduced operators online.
