= Greedy reduced bases and POD
:stem: latexmath

Read xref:reduction/pod.adoc[POD] and xref:certification/residual-bound.adoc[residual certificates] first.
POD minimizes an average squared projection error for a declared snapshot distribution and state metric.
A reduced-basis greedy algorithm instead seeks a space that controls the largest estimated reduced-solve error on a finite training set.
Neither criterion alone guarantees accuracy outside that set.

== Certified selection

Let the discrete model be coercive, with reduced space stem:[V_r] and a computable certificate:

[stem]
++++
\|u_h(\mu)-u_r(\mu)\|_V\leq\Delta_r(\mu)
=\frac{\|f_\mu-a_\mu(u_r,\cdot)\|_{V_h'}}{\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}(\mu)}.
++++

Here the numerator is a dual norm on the *full discrete test space*, not the norm of the reduced-system residual.
For the candidate prediction, form stem:[\rho=f-AZa], solve stem:[Gz_R=\rho], and use

[stem]
++++
\Delta_r(\mu)=\frac{\sqrt{\rho^Tz_R}}{\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}(\mu)}.
++++

The xref:foundations/riesz.adoc[Riesz representation notes] explain why this solve computes the supremum in the dual norm.
This direct evaluation is sufficient for understanding selection; it has full-dimensional cost.
The xref:certification/residual-bound.adoc#efficient-residual[offline residual factor] is optional advanced reading for accelerating the scan.
A valid but pessimistic estimator can select differently and stop later; use xref:certification/residual-bound.adoc#effectivity[effectivity] to study its sharpness on reference cases.

. Fix a training set, tolerance, maximum rank and initial parameter independently of the test set.
. Solve the full model at the initial parameter and normalize its snapshot in the chosen metric.
. Solve reduced systems and evaluate certificates at every training parameter.
. Select a maximizer of the certificate; stop if the maximum is below tolerance.
. Compute its full snapshot, remove its projection onto the current space, and normalize the remainder.
. Reorthogonalize and reject a numerically dependent direction; repeat.

[.prop,caption=""]
.What the stopping test establishes
====
If the certificate is valid at every training parameter and its maximum is at most the tolerance, every training reduced solution meets that tolerance.
This statement does not bound the error at an unseen parameter without additional regularity and sampling arguments.
====

A vanishing enrichment vector with a nonzero certificate may indicate estimator pessimism, cancellation or an insufficient stopping tolerance.
Report this condition instead of dividing by a tiny norm.


[[greedy-algorithm]]
=== Algorithm — Estimator-driven enrichment

At each rank, the certificate scan selects the parameter before its full solution is computed.
The two projection passes improve orthogonality in floating-point arithmetic.
Use a declared dependence threshold to distinguish enrichment failure from successful error-based termination.

[.pseudocode]
....
\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Certified greedy reduced-basis construction}
\begin{algorithmic}
\INPUT Training set $\Xi$; seed $\mu_0$; metric $G$; tolerance $\varepsilon$; maximum rank $r_{\max}$
\INPUT Valid certificate $\Delta_r(\mu)$ and dependence threshold $\delta$
\STATE Solve for $u_0=u_h(\mu_0)$
\REQUIRE $\|u_0\|_G>\delta$
\STATE $Z\leftarrow u_0/\|u_0\|_G$; $r\leftarrow 1$
\WHILE{true}
  \STATE Build the reduced operators and certificate data for $Z$
  \FORALL{$\mu\in\Xi$}
    \STATE Solve the reduced problem and compute $\Delta_r(\mu)$
  \ENDFOR
  \STATE $\mu_*\leftarrow\operatorname{argmax}_{\mu\in\Xi}\Delta_r(\mu)$
  \IF{$\Delta_r(\mu_*)\leq\varepsilon$}
    \RETURN $Z$ and the certified training-set stopping value
  \ENDIF
  \IF{$r=r_{\max}$}
    \RETURN $Z$ with the unmet tolerance reported
  \ENDIF
  \STATE Solve for $v=u_h(\mu_*)$
  \FOR{$j=1$ \TO $2$}
    \STATE $v\leftarrow v-Z(Z^TGv)$
  \ENDFOR
  \IF{$\|v\|_G\leq\delta$}
    \RETURN $Z$ with a numerical-dependence warning
  \ENDIF
  \STATE Append $v/\|v\|_G$ to $Z$; $r\leftarrow r+1$
\ENDWHILE
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
....

A reported tolerance concerns the finite training set and the stated certificate assumptions.
The xref:course-rom::labs/session04-greedy.adoc[practical] separates construction snapshots from full reference solutions used for diagnostic plots. Reference solves are not required by the selection criterion; optional POD comparisons have their own snapshot budget.

== Compare fairly

Use identical parameter domains, metrics, ranks and held-out evaluation points.
For POD, state the snapshot weights: uniform spacing in a parameter and uniform spacing in its logarithm describe different empirical distributions.
Compare projection error separately from Galerkin solve error; the POD optimality identity applies to the former on the training snapshots.
Greedy may use a different set of full solves, so account for offline solve counts as well as online accuracy.

Estimator-driven greedy normally needs one additional full solve per accepted direction.
POD first needs the chosen snapshot ensemble, then its factorization.
The training scan can still be expensive even if each certificate is independent of the full dimension.
Affine projection of the prediction operators is core course material.
Precomputing a residual factor to accelerate the certificate is the optional extension in the certificate notes.

== From stationary greedy to POD-greedy trajectories

For a parabolic model, one parameter produces a whole time trajectory, not a single state. Start from the xref:certification/extensions.adoc#parabolic-prediction[reduced backward-Euler prediction] and the xref:certification/extensions.adoc#transient-bound[accumulated transient residual bound]. Keep the time grid, input and initial condition fixed during each declared parameter experiment.

The outer greedy chooses the parameter with the largest trajectory certificate. A full solve at that parameter returns snapshots at many times. Adding all of them would enlarge the basis too quickly. Instead remove the current mass-orthogonal projection and apply POD to the remaining snapshots:

[stem]
++++
E=[u_h^1-Z(Z^TMu_h^1),\ldots,u_h^K-Z(Z^TMu_h^K)],\qquad Z^TMZ=I.
++++

Use snapshot weights stem:[w_k=\Delta t_k/T] (equal weights on the uniform grid) and retain one or a few leading *defect* modes. Each new mode is spatial; time enters through the snapshot collection and reduced time integration. This is not a separate temporal-basis construction. The same parameter may be selected repeatedly because one trajectory can contain several independent spatial directions.

[.pseudocode]
....
\begin{algorithm}
\caption{POD-greedy for parametrized time trajectories}
\begin{algorithmic}
\INPUT Training parameters $\Xi$, seed $\mu_0$, tolerance $\varepsilon$, rank budget $r_{\max}$
\INPUT Mass metric $M$, time weights $w_k$, valid trajectory certificate $D_r(\mu)$
\STATE Compute the full trajectory at $\mu_0$ and retain its leading mass-POD mode in $Z$
\WHILE{true}
  \STATE Project affine mass, stiffness, source and output components for $Z$
  \STATE Compute reduced trajectories and $D_r(\mu)$ for every $\mu\in\Xi$
  \STATE $\mu_*\leftarrow\operatorname{argmax}_{\mu\in\Xi}D_r(\mu)$
  \IF{$D_r(\mu_*)\leq\varepsilon$}
    \RETURN $Z$ and the certified training stopping value
  \ENDIF
  \IF{$\dim(Z)=r_{\max}$}
    \RETURN $Z$ with unmet tolerance reported
  \ENDIF
  \STATE Solve the full trajectory at $\mu_*$, or reuse it if already cached
  \STATE Form defects $E_k=u_h^k-Z(Z^TMu_h^k)$
  \STATE Apply time-weighted mass-POD to the defects
  \STATE Retain resolved leading modes within the remaining rank budget
  \STATE Reorthogonalize in $M$, reject dependence and append the new modes to $Z$
\ENDWHILE
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
....

The POD metric and the residual-certification metric need not coincide. In the xref:course-rom::labs/session13-thermal-fin.adoc#prediction[thermal-fin practical], POD uses the mass matrix stem:[M]; the Riesz residual uses a fixed reference stiffness stem:[G=A(Bi_{\rm ref})]. The coercivity lower bound must refer to stem:[G]. A steady residual omits the storage term and cannot replace the transient certificate.

Nested spaces imply nonincreasing best-projection errors on a fixed snapshot ensemble. The integrated transient Galerkin prediction is not that projection, so its error need not decrease at every enrichment. Plot both quantities and the certificate against the *actual spatial dimension*, recording full trajectories separately. If a prototype selects with exact reference errors instead of certificates, label it reference-error-driven and account for the required full solves.
