= DEIM: POD-based empirical interpolation
:stem: latexmath

Read xref:hyper-reduction/index.adoc[non-affinity and EIM] first.
DEIM constructs a POD basis of source or nonlinear-term snapshots, then chooses interpolation coordinates for that basis.
The state basis and nonlinear-term basis have different roles and generally different dimensions.

== Construction

Let the columns of stem:[U_m] be Euclidean-orthonormal POD modes of the term to approximate.
Choose the largest-magnitude entry of the first mode.
For each subsequent mode, interpolate it using previous modes and points, then choose the largest-magnitude residual entry.
The new point cannot repeat an earlier one while the residual is nonzero.

[stem]
++++
D_m f=U_m(P_m^TU_m)^{-1}P_m^Tf.
++++

Use a linear solve; the inverse in the formula is not an instruction to compute an explicit inverse in code.
Interpolation is exact at the selected coordinates and on the span of the retained modes.

[.prop,caption=""]
.Euclidean error bound
====
For orthonormal stem:[U_m], distinct selected coordinates and nonsingular interpolation matrix:

[stem]
++++
\|f-D_m f\|_2\leq
\|(P_m^TU_m)^{-1}\|_2\,
\|(I-U_mU_m^T)f\|_2.
++++

The full-rank case is exact. The proof for stem:[0<m<n] is given below.
The bound uses the inverse norm, not just a condition number or the snapshot singular values.
====

[[deim-bound-proof]]
=== What the amplification factor means

For stem:[T=P_m^TU_m], the induced Euclidean norm of its inverse is

[stem]
++++
\|T^{-1}\|_2=\frac1{\sigma_{\min}(T)},\qquad
\kappa_2(T)=\frac{\sigma_{\max}(T)}{\sigma_{\min}(T)}.
++++

The smallest singular value is the minimum of stem:[\|Tc\|_2] over unit vectors stem:[c].
A small value means that a unit combination of retained modes is barely visible at the selected coordinates.
The inverse must amplify that measurement direction to recover its coefficients.
Compute this factor by an SVD of the small matrix; the condition number alone is a different quantity.

[.proof,caption=""]
.Proof of the Euclidean bound (supplementary derivation)
====
Write stem:[U=U_m], stem:[P=P_m] and stem:[D=UT^{-1}P^T].
Since stem:[DU=U], the error is stem:[(I-D)(I-UU^T)f].
Complete stem:[U] to an orthogonal basis with stem:[U_\perp].
In those coordinates the projector and its complement have the block forms

[stem]
++++
D\sim\begin{bmatrix}I&B\\0&0\end{bmatrix},\qquad
I-D\sim\begin{bmatrix}0&-B\\0&I\end{bmatrix},\qquad
B=T^{-1}P^TU_\perp.
++++

Both norms equal stem:[\sqrt{1+\|B\|_2^2}]: multiply the nonzero block row by its transpose for the first and the block column by its transpose in the opposite order for the second.
Also stem:[\|D\|_2=\|T^{-1}\|_2], since stem:[U] preserves vector lengths, stem:[P^T] cannot increase them, and inputs of the form stem:[Pv] attain every unit measurement direction.
Applying this common norm to the orthogonal residual proves the bound.
====

A small POD training tail does not by itself guarantee a small test error or a well-conditioned sampling matrix.
Record the held-out projection error, interpolation error and inverse norm separately.
For a weighted state metric, first specify which space and norm the nonlinear snapshots inhabit; the Euclidean bound cannot simply be relabeled as a mass-norm bound.


[[deim-algorithm]]
=== Algorithm — DEIM point selection

The source basis is fixed before point selection.
At iteration stem:[k], subtract the interpolant of the new mode using only the preceding modes and points.
For independent modes the exact residual is nonzero; a small computed residual needs a numerical-rank check.

[.pseudocode]
....
\begin{algorithm}
\caption{DEIM selection for a POD source basis}
\begin{algorithmic}
\INPUT Orthonormal basis $U\in\mathbb R^{n\times m}$ and dependence threshold $\delta$
\STATE $p_1\leftarrow\operatorname{argmax}_i|U_{i1}|$; $p\leftarrow[p_1]$
\FOR{$k=2$ \TO $m$}
  \STATE Solve $U[p,1:k-1]c=U[p,k]$
  \STATE $e\leftarrow U_{:,k}-U_{:,1:k-1}c$
  \STATE $i_*\leftarrow\operatorname{argmax}_i|e_i|$
  \IF{$|e_{i_*}|\leq\delta$}
    \RETURN Numerical dependence: review the requested basis rank
  \ENDIF
  \STATE Append $i_*$ to $p$
\ENDFOR
\STATE $T\leftarrow U[p,:]$
\OUTPUT Selected rows $p$ and interpolation matrix $T$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
....

Online, solve stem:[Tc=f[p]] and apply a precomputed reduced lifting operator.
Check the inverse norm of stem:[T] alongside test errors; point selection does not eliminate amplification.

== Nonlinear reduced systems

With a state basis stem:[Z_r], precompute the reduced lifting operator:

[stem]
++++
Z_r^T f(Z_r a;\mu)\approx
\underbrace{Z_r^TU_m(P_m^TU_m)^{-1}}_{C_{rm}}
P_m^T f(Z_r a;\mu).
++++

Efficient selected evaluation depends on the model.
For pointwise nonlinearities, selected state entries can use precomputed rows of the state basis.
For a discretized PDE stencil, neighboring degrees of freedom are also needed.
A global integral or a black-box routine returning a full vector may prevent the assumed saving.
