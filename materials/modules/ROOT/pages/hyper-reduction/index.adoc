= Non-affinity and empirical interpolation
:stem: latexmath

An affine decomposition separates parameter dependence from spatial operators:

[stem]
++++
A(\mu)=\sum_{q=1}^{Q_a}\theta_q(\mu)A_q,
\qquad f(\mu)=\sum_{q=1}^{Q_f}\eta_q(\mu)f_q.
++++

The xref:reduction/galerkin.adoc#affine-example[worked affine prediction example] shows exactly which arrays are stored and used at a query.
For small fixed numbers of terms, projected matrices and vectors are assembled offline.
A moving Gaussian source or a nonlinear constitutive law generally lacks such a small exact decomposition.
Evaluating the entire source or nonlinear residual online can then dominate the reduced solve.
Empirical interpolation constructs a separable approximation whose coefficients are determined from selected evaluations.

== EIM: build the basis and interpolation points together

For a finite training collection of scalar fields, start with an empty interpolant.
At step stem:[m], find a training field with maximum infinity-norm interpolation residual, then a grid point where that residual is largest in magnitude.
Divide the residual by its signed value at that point to obtain the next basis function.
Preserve previous points and functions.

[stem]
++++
I_m f=Q_m c,\qquad (P_m^TQ_m)c=P_m^Tf.
++++

Here columns of stem:[P_m] are coordinate vectors selecting the points.
The residual vanishes at previously selected points, so the interpolation matrix is lower triangular with unit diagonal in exact arithmetic.
This is an algebraic interpolation property; it does not imply that the matrix is well conditioned.
Stop when the maximum training residual meets tolerance or becomes numerically negligible.

[.prop,caption=""]
.Error relative to best approximation
====
For a bounded interpolation operator that is the identity on its range:

[stem]
++++
\|f-I_m f\|_\infty\leq
(1+\|I_m\|_{\infty\to\infty})
\inf_{v\in\operatorname{range}(Q_m)}\|f-v\|_\infty.
++++

For any such stem:[v], write the error as stem:[(I-I_m)(f-v)] and use the operator norm.
For a discrete grid, this constant can be computed.
Set stem:[T=P_m^TQ_m] and obtain stem:[D=Q_mT^{-1}] by a solve with stem:[T^T].
Because stem:[P_m^T] only selects distinct coordinates,

[stem]
++++
\|I_m\|_{\infty\to\infty}=\max_i\sum_{j=1}^m|D_{ij}|.
++++

This is the largest absolute row sum, often called the discrete Lebesgue constant: the triangle inequality proves the upper bound, and choosing signs to match a maximizing row attains it.
It measures the worst amplification of a field perturbation, beyond the best-approximation error.
The discrete norm concerns this grid; a continuum claim needs control between grid points.
====


[[eim-algorithm]]
=== Algorithm — Discrete EIM construction

Collect the training fields as columns of stem:[F].
The joint maximum over residual entries is equivalent to selecting a worst training field and then its worst grid point.
The selected index list stem:[p] determines the rows used to solve for the interpolant coefficients.

[.pseudocode]
....
\begin{algorithm}
\caption{EIM on a finite grid and training collection}
\begin{algorithmic}
\INPUT Training matrix $F\in\mathbb R^{n\times N_s}$; tolerance $\varepsilon$; maximum size $M$
\STATE $Q\leftarrow[\,]$; $p\leftarrow[\,]$; $m\leftarrow0$
\WHILE{$m<M$}
  \IF{$m=0$}
    \STATE $E\leftarrow F$
  \ELSE
    \STATE Solve $Q[p,:]C=F[p,:]$
    \STATE $E\leftarrow F-QC$
  \ENDIF
  \STATE $(i_*,j_*)\leftarrow\operatorname{argmax}_{i,j}|E_{ij}|$
  \IF{$|E_{i_*j_*}|\leq\varepsilon$}
    \RETURN $Q,p$ and the attained training residual
  \ENDIF
  \STATE $q\leftarrow E_{:,j_*}/E_{i_*j_*}$
  \STATE Append $q$ to $Q$ and $i_*$ to $p$; $m\leftarrow m+1$
\ENDWHILE
\OUTPUT EIM basis $Q$, selected points $p$ and size $m$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
....

For a new field, evaluate only its selected entries, solve stem:[Q[p,:]c=f[p]], and use the coefficients in the precomputed reduced expression.
Forming the full interpolated field costs additional full-dimensional work.
If the maximum size is reached, recompute the final training residual before claiming that a tolerance has been met.

== Coefficient approximation and PDE error

Interpolating a PDE coefficient creates an additional consistency error, separate from Galerkin truncation.
For a right-hand-side approximation with a fixed coercive operator, the full solution perturbation satisfies:

[stem]
++++
\|u_h-\widetilde u_h\|_V\leq
\frac{\|f-\widetilde f\|_{V_h'}}{\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}}.
++++

Subtract the two equations with the same operator to obtain stem:[a_\mu(u_h-\widetilde u_h,v)=(f-\widetilde f)(v)].
The xref:certification/residual-bound.adoc#certified-bound[coercivity proof] applies with the forcing discrepancy as its right-hand-side functional.
Compute that functional's norm with the same xref:foundations/riesz.adoc#matrix-riesz[Riesz map]; a Euclidean source-vector error is not automatically a dual norm for another metric.

For an interpolated operator, positivity/coercivity can be lost. Check stability of the approximated operator and include its consistency residual; the certificate for the original operator is not automatically unchanged.
The course practical starts with a non-affine source to isolate interpolation from this additional difficulty.
