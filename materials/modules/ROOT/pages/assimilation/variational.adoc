= PBDW: background space and data-driven correction
:stem: latexmath

Read xref:assimilation/geim.adoc[observations and GEIM] first.
PBDW combines a low-dimensional background space with an update determined by measurements.
The background is a space of plausible states; its coefficients are inferred from data, not supplied by the unknown true state.

== Riesz representatives and stability

Each sensor is a bounded linear functional represented in the state inner product by a vector.
Read the xref:foundations/riesz.adoc#matrix-riesz[matrix derivation] first: applying a sensor and taking the inner product with its representative must give the same value for every state.
For state metric stem:[G] and measurement matrix stem:[H], solve stem:[GW=H^T] and define:

[stem]
++++
W=G^{-1}H^T,\quad \mathcal W_M=\operatorname{range}(W),\quad
A=HW=H G^{-1}H^T,\quad B=HZ.
++++

Columns of stem:[Z] span the background stem:[X_N]. Columns of stem:[W] are the sensor Riesz representatives: they are not the GEIM functions stem:[\rho_j]. The matrices stem:[A=HW] and stem:[B=HZ] below are PBDW saddle blocks, not the PDE operator stem:[A(\mu)] or the EIM/GEIM interpolation matrix stem:[\mathbb B_M].
The identity between applying a functional and taking an inner product with its Riesz representative is:

[stem]
++++
Hu=W^TGu.
++++

For example, with stem:[G=hI], point sensors have representatives scaled by stem:[1/h].
For density rows where the measurement matrix already includes stem:[h], the Riesz columns have no additional quadrature factor.

Assume the sensors are linearly independent, their number is at least the background rank, and stem:[HZ] has full column rank.
The background observability constant is:

[stem]
++++
\beta=\inf_{z\in X_N\setminus\{0\}}
\frac{\|P_{\mathcal W_M}z\|_X}{\|z\|_X}\in(0,1].
++++

To compute it, factor stem:[G=LL^T] and map both spaces into Euclidean coordinates.
Obtain orthonormal column bases from thin QR factorizations:

[stem]
++++
L^TZ=U_X R_X,\qquad L^TW=U_{\mathcal W} R_{\mathcal W}.
++++

A unit background vector has transformed coordinates stem:[U_Xc] with stem:[\|c\|_2=1].
Projection onto the transformed sensor space gives stem:[U_{\mathcal W}U_{\mathcal W}^TU_Xc], whose norm is stem:[\|U_{\mathcal W}^TU_Xc\|_2].
Consequently,

[stem]
++++
\beta=\min_{\|c\|_2=1}\|U_{\mathcal W}^TU_Xc\|_2
=\sigma_{\min}(U_{\mathcal W}^TU_X).
++++

This derives the computational recipe directly from the definition.
For stem:[G=I], a one-dimensional background along stem:[(1,0)^T] and a unit sensor direction stem:[(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)^T] give stem:[\beta=|\cos\theta|].
A perpendicular sensor sees none of that background direction; near-perpendicularity makes recovery unstable.
This result is invariant under nonsingular changes of coordinates within either space.
The smallest singular value of the unnormalized matrix stem:[HZ] is not this constant.
A small beta indicates weakly observed background directions.

== Minimum correction formulation

For exact observations, find a background component and a minimum-norm correction satisfying the data:

[stem]
++++
\min_{z\in X_N,\eta\in X}\tfrac12\|\eta\|_X^2
\quad\text{subject to}\quad H(z+\eta)=y.
++++

[.prop,caption=""]
.Saddle system
====
The minimizing update lies in the Riesz space and is orthogonal to the background.
Writing the update as stem:[Wd] and background as stem:[Za] gives:

[stem]
++++
\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\B^T&0\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}d\\a\end{bmatrix}
=\begin{bmatrix}y\\0\end{bmatrix},\qquad
\widehat u=Za+Wd.
++++

Introduce the Lagrangian, using a minus sign for the multiplier:

[stem]
++++
\mathcal L(\eta,a,d)=\tfrac12\eta^TG\eta-d^T\bigl(H(Za+\eta)-y\bigr).
++++

Stationarity with respect to the update, background coefficients and multiplier gives, respectively,

[stem]
++++
G\eta=H^Td,\qquad B^Td=0,\qquad H(Za+\eta)=y.
++++

The first equation gives stem:[\eta=Wd], and the last becomes the upper block equation.
Furthermore stem:[Z^TG\eta=B^Td=0], proving orthogonality to the background.
To check uniqueness, use a homogeneous right-hand side and multiply the upper equation by stem:[d^T].
The lower equation cancels the mixed term, leaving stem:[d^TAd=0].
Independent sensors make stem:[A] positive definite, so stem:[d=0]; full column rank of stem:[B] then gives stem:[a=0].
====

PBDW reproduces background states from exact data under these assumptions.
An arbitrary discrepancy is not fully recoverable: any component invisible to all sensors remains unidentified.
Adding observations can help, but duplicate or nearly dependent sensors add little stable information.

== Noisy measurements

For equally scaled, independent noise components, relax the constraint:

[stem]
++++
\min_{z\in X_N,\eta\in X}
\|H(z+\eta)-y\|_2^2+\xi\|\eta\|_X^2,\qquad \xi>0.
++++

The same representation yields the saddle matrix with stem:[A+\xi I] in the upper-left block.
The residual is then stem:[-\xi d]; exact data interpolation is no longer the objective.
Here is how the regularized equations follow.
Let stem:[r_y=H(Za+\eta)-y]. Differentiation gives stem:[H^Tr_y+\xi G\eta=0] and stem:[B^Tr_y=0].
Setting stem:[d=-r_y/\xi] yields stem:[\eta=Wd] and the stated block equations.

For correlated or unequal noise with positive definite covariance stem:[R=CC^T], *whitening* means solving

[stem]
++++
\widetilde y=C^{-1}y,\quad\widetilde H=C^{-1}H,\quad
\operatorname{Cov}(C^{-1}\varepsilon)=I.
++++

Use these transformed measurements and sensors in the same formulation.
Their squared residual norm is the noise-weighted least-squares quantity stem:[r_y^TR^{-1}r_y].
Large-variance measurement directions receive less weight; correlated directions are first decorrelated.
Use triangular solves, and recompute the Riesz and saddle matrices with the transformed operator.
See xref:assimilation/kalman.adoc#covariance-basics[covariance and linear transformations] for the identity behind whitening.
Choose regularization using prior noise knowledge or separate validation experiments, not the held-out truth being scored.


[[pbdw-algorithm]]
=== Algorithm — Exact or regularized PBDW

Apply the same saddle construction to exact data with zero regularization or to whitened noisy data with positive regularization.
The rank conditions are checked before attempting a solve.
The regularized problem uses the measurement scaling described above.

[.pseudocode]
....
\begin{algorithm}
\caption{PBDW reconstruction}
\begin{algorithmic}
\INPUT Metric $G$; background basis $Z$; independent sensors $H$; data $y$; regularization $\xi\geq0$
\REQUIRE $G$ is positive definite and $\operatorname{rank}(HZ)=N$
\STATE Solve $GW=H^T$
\STATE $A\leftarrow HW$; $B\leftarrow HZ$
\STATE Solve $(A+\xi I)d+Ba=y$ together with $B^Td=0$
\STATE $\eta\leftarrow Wd$; $\widehat u\leftarrow Za+\eta$
\STATE Check $Z^TG\eta=0$ and $H\widehat u-y=-\xi d$ within numerical tolerance
\OUTPUT Reconstructed state $\widehat u$ and update $\eta$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
....

Precompute and factor the saddle matrix when the spaces, sensors and regularization remain fixed.
Recompute it when any of those choices change; the observation vector alone is the varying right-hand side.
