= Ensemble Kalman filtering and reduced forecasts
:stem: latexmath

Read xref:assimilation/kalman.adoc[the Kalman filter] first.
The EnKF propagates an ensemble through a possibly nonlinear model and estimates forecast covariances from its anomalies.
It is not an exact nonlinear Bayesian filter; even an infinite ensemble need not make a non-Gaussian posterior Gaussian.

As in the Kalman chapter, stem:[x_i] is the coordinate vector of ensemble member stem:[i], and superscripts stem:[f,a] mean forecast and analysis. The matrix stem:[X] below collects members; it is not the function space stem:[X] of the ROM chapters. Primes on ensemble matrices denote centered anomalies, not time derivatives. Their array shapes distinguish these declared objects.

== Forecast and sample covariance

Propagate each analysis member, adding independent process noise when that is part of the specified model.
For stem:[N_e>1] members, let stem:[X] contain forecast states and stem:[Y] their predicted observations.
Subtract the respective ensemble means to obtain anomaly matrices.

[stem]
++++
\begin{aligned}
C_{xy}&=\frac{X'Y'^T}{N_e-1}, & C_{yy}&=\frac{Y'Y'^T}{N_e-1},\\
K&=C_{xy}(C_{yy}+R)^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
++++

For linear observations, predicted observations are simply stem:[HX].
The anomaly matrix stem:[X'\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal N\times N_e}] has columns stem:[x_i-\bar x] and satisfies stem:[X'\mathbf1=0].
Thus its rank, and the rank of its sample covariance, cannot exceed stem:[N_e-1].
The denominator stems from estimating the mean using those same samples: for independent samples with covariance stem:[P],

[stem]
++++
\mathbb E\!\left[\sum_i(x_i-\bar x)(x_i-\bar x)^T\right]
=N_eP-N_e\operatorname{Cov}(\bar x)=(N_e-1)P.
++++

This explains the usual unbiased sample-covariance normalization.
Filter members become dependent through their shared gain and observations; the sample formula does not make the resulting filter exact.
Small ensembles can produce spurious distant correlations and underestimate uncertainty.

== Stochastic analysis

[stem]
++++
x_i^a=x_i^f+K\bigl(y+\varepsilon_i-h(x_i^f)\bigr),
\qquad\varepsilon_i\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}\sim\mathcal N(0,R).
++++

The perturbations are independent of forecast errors.
For linear observations and a fixed forecast ensemble, the gain is fixed before drawing perturbations.
Let stem:[E'] be the matrix of centered observation perturbations.
Centering the member updates gives

[stem]
++++
X^{\prime a}=(I-KH)X^{\prime f}+KE'.
++++

Conditionally on the forecast ensemble, the perturbations have mean zero, the mixed terms have zero expectation, and their sample covariance has expectation stem:[R].
Consequently the expected analysis sample covariance is

[stem]
++++
\mathbb E_\varepsilon[P_e^a\mid X^f]
=(I-KH)P_e^f(I-KH)^T+KRK^T.
++++

This recovers the observation-noise term of the Joseph formula in expectation.
Without perturbations the second term is missing.
In the xref:assimilation/kalman.adoc[scalar example], forecast variance 2 and gain stem:[2/3] would leave variance stem:[2/9] without perturbations, compared with the target stem:[2/3].
The missing stem:[4/9] is exactly stem:[KRK^T].
A finite ensemble also has a random perturbation mean, of covariance stem:[R/N_e]; this adds sampling variability to the analysis mean.
For nonlinear observations, the sample cross-covariance update remains an approximation, not the exact Gaussian conditioning formula for a linear observation.
Deterministic square-root EnKFs use a different anomaly transform and are outside this course's required implementation.

Multiplicative inflation scales anomalies about their mean; it must not scale the mean state.
For anomaly multiplier stem:[\lambda], use

[stem]
++++
x_i\leftarrow\bar x+\lambda(x_i-\bar x),\qquad P_e\leftarrow\lambda^2P_e.
++++

To multiply covariance by a specified factor, multiply anomalies by its square root.
Localization modifies sample correlations using additional spatial assumptions; it is not just increasing the ensemble size.
Tune such choices with separate validation data and retain an untouched test experiment.


[[enkf-algorithm]]
=== Algorithm — One stochastic EnKF cycle

The model propagates every member before sample covariances are formed.
Use independent process-noise draws according to the stated forecast model and independent observation perturbations with covariance stem:[R].
The following cycle omits optional inflation and localization so that the basic stochastic update is explicit.

[.pseudocode]
....
\begin{algorithm}
\caption{One stochastic ensemble Kalman step}
\begin{algorithmic}
\INPUT Analysis ensemble $\{x_i^a\}_{i=1}^{N_e}$; forecast map $\mathcal F$; observation map $h$; data $y$; covariance $R$
\REQUIRE $N_e>1$ and a declared process-noise model
\FOR{$i=1$ \TO $N_e$}
  \STATE Draw independent process noise $w_i$
  \STATE $x_i^f\leftarrow\mathcal F(x_i^a)+w_i$
  \STATE $y_i^f\leftarrow h(x_i^f)$
\ENDFOR
\STATE Assemble $X=[x_i^f]$, $Y=[y_i^f]$ and their column means $\bar x,\bar y$
\STATE $X'\leftarrow X-\bar x\mathbf1^T$; $Y'\leftarrow Y-\bar y\mathbf1^T$
\STATE $C_{xy}\leftarrow X'Y'^T/(N_e-1)$; $C_{yy}\leftarrow Y'Y'^T/(N_e-1)$
\STATE Solve $(C_{yy}+R)K^T=C_{xy}^T$
\FOR{$i=1$ \TO $N_e$}
  \STATE Draw $\varepsilon_i\sim\mathcal N(0,R)$ independently
  \STATE $x_i^a\leftarrow x_i^f+K(y+\varepsilon_i-y_i^f)$
\ENDFOR
\OUTPUT Analysis ensemble and its sample mean/covariance
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
....

For a deterministic forecast without modeled process noise, set every process-noise draw to zero.
For a coefficient-space ROM filter, replace the state by its coefficient vector and use the reduced forecast and observation operators derived next.

== Coupling to a reduced model

Start from the semi-discrete evolution problem, with fixed basis stem:[Z]:

[stem]
++++
M\dot u+A(\mu)u=f g(t),\qquad u_N(t)=Za(t).
++++

Substitute the reduced state and test with stem:[Z]. Since the basis is time independent, its derivative is stem:[Z\dot a].
This yields

[stem]
++++
M_N\dot a+A_N(\mu)a=f_N g(t),\qquad
M_N=Z^TMZ,\quad A_N=Z^TAZ,\quad f_N=Z^Tf.
++++

Implicit Euler replaces the derivative by the backward difference and evaluates the stiffness term and forcing at the new time:

[stem]
++++
(M_N+\Delta t A_N)a_{k+1}=M_ra_k+\Delta t f_N g_{k+1},
\qquad M_N=Z^TMZ,\quad A_N=Z^TAZ.
++++

If stem:[A(\mu)=\sum_q\Theta_q^a(\mu)A_q], store stem:[Z^TA_qZ], stem:[M_N] and stem:[f_N] offline exactly as in xref:reduction/galerkin.adoc#affine-offline-online[affine prediction].
Online, combine only reduced stiffness terms. For fixed parameter and time step, factor the reduced time-stepping matrix once and reuse its factors for every ensemble member and time step.
If the parameter changes, update that small matrix and its factorization.

Substituting stem:[u_N=Za] in the sensor equation gives stem:[y=HZ a+v].
Precompute the observation operator stem:[H_N=HZ] and reduced scalar outputs.
Filtering the coefficients costs much less than forming full-state covariances, but all ensemble corrections remain in the chosen reduced span.
Inflation cannot create a direction absent from that span.
Account separately for reduction error, parameter/model mismatch, observation noise and finite-ensemble variability.
A small spread may reflect a restrictive subspace rather than reliable predictions.
