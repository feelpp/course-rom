= Session 2 — Galerkin reduction and POD
:navtitle: 2 · Galerkin and POD

*Two-hour cours intégré · explanation, derivation and Python practical*

By the end, you should be able to construct a snapshot space, solve a projected system, evaluate an output on unseen parameters, and identify which operations are offline or online.
Reduction supplies faster predictions for our digital-twin example; assimilation will later connect those predictions to observations.

== Prepare

Recall PCA from the S1 Data Processing course: centering, scores and explained variance. This S3 session connects those concepts to POD and predictive reduced models.

Review the xref:sessions/01-digital-twins.adoc[opening model] and confirm that xref:setup.adoc[Jupyter setup] works.
Required notes: xref:rom::reduction/galerkin.adoc[Galerkin reduced models] and xref:rom::reduction/pod.adoc[POD].
Use xref:rom::notation.adoc[shared notation] when a symbol or norm is unclear.

== During the session

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Minutes |Activity
|0–15 |Connect S1 PCA to S3 snapshot reduction; recall the parameter-to-output problem.
|15–35 |Derive Galerkin equations and the offline/online decomposition.
|35–60 |Practical: snapshots, singular values and reduced dimension.
|60–75 |Discuss centering, the physical metric, projection optimality and held-out validation.
|75–110 |Practical: reduced solves, state/output errors and rank comparisons.
|110–120 |Compare findings and motivate an error estimate without a full solve.
|===

Open the xref:labs/session02-galerkin-pod.adoc[executable practical and notebook download].
The practical's 60 minutes of student work are part of these two hours.

== Checkpoint

Keep your rank/error table, one held-out error plot and a short explanation of offline versus online operations.
Be able to explain why a POD training tail is different from a reduced-solve error and why a projected sensor model does not itself assimilate data.
