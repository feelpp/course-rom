= Session 2 — Galerkin reduction and POD
:navtitle: 2 · Galerkin and POD

*Two-hour cours intégré · explanation, derivation and Python practical*

By the end, you should be able to construct snapshot and POD bases, solve a projected system, evaluate an output and residual bound on unseen parameters, interpret effectivity, and test the conditioning result.
Reduction supplies faster predictions for our digital-twin example; assimilation will later connect those predictions to observations.

== Prepare

Recall PCA from the S1 Data Processing course: singular values and explained variance. Here POD applies a related factorization to solution snapshots, while Galerkin determines new predictions from the governing equation.

Review the xref:sessions/01-digital-twins.adoc[opening model] and confirm that xref:setup.adoc[Jupyter setup] works.
Required notes: xref:rom::reduction/galerkin.adoc[Galerkin reduced models], xref:rom::reduction/pod.adoc[POD], and xref:rom::certification/residual-bound.adoc[Residual bounds and effectivity] through the reaction–diffusion practical.
The xref:rom::reduction/galerkin.adoc#affine-example[small-matrix example] is part of the required prediction methodology.
Use xref:rom::notation.adoc[shared notation] when a symbol or norm is unclear.

== During the session

Open the xref:labs/galerkin-sampling.adoc[integrated practical] and download its notebook from the page toolbar. The 1D full-order model has two parameters—diffusivity and right-endpoint flux—and the output of interest is mean temperature. The supplied plots vary one parameter at a time; complete the two-dimensional sampling, basis, projection and certification cells during the session.

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Minutes |Activity
|0–10 |Explore the 1D model, right-endpoint flux, mean-temperature output and fixed reference-energy metric.
|10–30 |Build structured and random sample pairs stem:[(\mu,g)]; orthonormalize their snapshot spans.
|30–45 |Project the affine operator using each snapshot basis and solve Galerkin systems at new parameters.
|45–60 |Construct rank-truncated POD bases as another choice of stem:[Z], then use the same projection code.
|60–85 |Compute the Riesz residual norm, coercivity constant, min-Theta lower bound and effectivity.
|85–110 |Compare held-out errors and test the reduced-matrix conditioning proposition.
|110–120 |Discuss results, offline/online work and the limits of one random draw.
|===

The xref:labs/session02-galerkin-pod.adoc[focused POD/PCA practical] remains available for additional work on centering and rank selection; it is not a second required activity within these two hours.

== Checkpoint

Keep your rank/error/effectivity table, one held-out error plot, the two sampling sets and a conditioning comparison.
Explain why a POD training tail is different from a reduced-solve error, why effectivity needs a full-order reference, and which parts of this teaching certificate still require full-dimensional work.
