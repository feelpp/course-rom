= Exercises and homework — Sensors, GEIM and PBDW
:stem: latexmath

For sessions 8–10. Suggested work outside class: 90 minutes.
Submit a notebook, the requested checks and at most two pages of conclusions.
Dates and marking rules are announced by the instructor.

== A. Riesz representation and identifiability (25 minutes)

For a symmetric positive-definite metric matrix and measurement matrix, derive the Riesz matrix:

[stem]
++++
Q=G^{-1}H^T.
++++

Check it numerically for point sensors and normalized averages in the metric from xref:labs/session09-pbdw.adoc[session 9].
Explain why inserting an extra quadrature factor would change the measurement model.
Construct a nonzero vector invisible to the sensors and show why exact observation fitting cannot determine its amplitude.

== B. Reconstruction and stability (30 minutes)

Run xref:labs/session08-geim.adoc[GEIM] and the PBDW practical.
For each method, distinguish the offline space, online observations and any use of synthetic truth.
For PBDW, verify data consistency, background/update orthogonality and exact recovery of a state in the background.
Compare well-spaced and clustered sensors using beta computed from whitened orthonormal bases.
Rescale background basis columns and verify beta invariance; explain why raw singular values need not remain unchanged.

== C. Noise and discrepancy (35 minutes)

Use xref:labs/session10-noise.adoc[the regularization experiment].
Keep one validation state and a separate held-out state, with independent noise seeds.
Choose regularization on validation data, freeze it and report test error and measurement misfit.
Repeat with a second sensor arrangement while preserving measurement units and noise variance.
Discuss why an exact interpolant may amplify noise and why a small data residual is not a state-error certificate.

== Evidence to submit

* Algebraic identities with explicit dimensions and the declared metric.
* One sensor/stability table and one noise/regularization plot.
* A statement of what is and is not identifiable from these observations.

Optional extension: whiten unequal or correlated observation errors before applying the regularized PBDW system.
