Session 2 — From snapshot bases to POD

Cours intégré · explanations and continuation of the same Python practical

The course route is sample → construct a basis → project with Galerkin → compress with POD. POD introduces a new way to construct the reduced space; the physical model and prediction code remain the same.

1. First stage — Sampling and Galerkin

Use the Reduced bases and Galerkin notes and the sampling/Galerkin practical. The model is a 1D thermal bar with diffusivity and right-endpoint flux as parameters. Keep the fixed reference-energy metric \(G=K+M\) and the mean-temperature output.

Complete the structured/random sampling, snapshot orthonormalization and Galerkin projection. The residual and conditioning tasks can be studied with these snapshot bases: they do not require POD. For certification, read Residual bounds and effectivity and the coercivity lower bound.

2. Next stage — Understand and use POD

Start with the POD notes: from SVD to parametrized PDE snapshots. Read through Use the POD basis in your Galerkin practical; the later sections are optional reading. The executed Python example computes thermal snapshots, constructs Euclidean and energy POD bases, reconstructs the profiles and reports training errors by rank. Recall the PCA studied in S1, but keep uncentered snapshots for this first ROM experiment.

Then download or reopen the same practical notebook, section 4. Use your existing snapshots and Galerkin functions; replace only the construction of \(Z\).

Minutes Suggested two-hour POD continuation

0–15

Discuss the completed sampling/Galerkin experiments and redundant snapshot directions.

15–35

Recall SVD and PCA; inspect the graphical rank-one example.

35–55

Explain the POD objective, reference-energy coordinates and singular-value tail.

55–100

Construct POD bases from both sampling sets; vary the rank and reuse the Galerkin solver.

100–120

Compare training projection, held-out state and output errors; discuss the rank choice.

3. Checkpoint

Bring a rank/error table and one plot for the same held-out pairs used previously. Explain why orthonormalization preserves a snapshot span while rank-truncated POD usually changes it, and why a small training tail is not a prediction certificate. Identify which computations are offline and which are needed for a new scalar-output prediction.

4. Further work

The optional PCA/centering experiment uses a different one-parameter model and an integral norm; it is supplementary, not the continuation of the two-parameter practical. The reduced-model homework includes that separate experiment. After POD and residual estimation, continue with greedy snapshot selection.
