Session 1 — Digital twins and a first numerical experiment

Thursday 10 September 2026 · 13:30–15:30 · Europe/Paris

1. What you will learn

  • Identify a physical system, its model, observations and a useful prediction or decision.

  • Run a supplied model and distinguish its state, parameter and output.

  • Explain why sparse measurements, model error and computational cost motivate the course.

2. Open these resources

  • Read: Digital twins: connecting models and observations.

  • Run: Download the student notebook (no external data files).

  • Prepare: Notebook environment and first-run instructions.

  • Explore: Notebook page with executed Python and example plots.

The notebook page shows the supplied starter experiment. Your practical work is to modify the downloaded notebook and explain the results.

3. During the session

Time Activity

13:30–13:55

Introduce a digital-twin scenario: physical system, observations, predictions and decisions.

13:55–14:05

Identify one quantity of interest and candidate observations; open the notebook.

14:05–14:25

Connect repeated simulation, reduction, accuracy assessment and mandatory assimilation.

14:25–14:55

Run the notebook experiment, vary a parameter and record a plot plus an interpretation.

14:55–15:10

Introduce reduced approximation and the upcoming POD/RB work.

15:10–15:30

Discuss what the experiment explains and what assimilation will add.

4. Practical: model, output and observations

The notebook adapts the supplied 1D reaction–diffusion solver from the tiny RB lab:

\[-\mu u''+u=1,\qquad u(0)=u(1)=0,\qquad \mu\in[0.1,10].\]

  1. Run the supplied cells from the top. Locate the parameter, state vector, mean output and sensor indices.

  2. Change mu_trial to at least two values in the allowed range. Describe how the profile and mean change.

  3. Compare the model’s sensor predictions with the synthetic observations. Distinguish a parameter mismatch from observation noise.

  4. Change the noise level or sensor locations and rerun the observation cells. Explain what information is lost or gained.

Save one state-profile plot, one model/observation plot, and short answers to the notebook questions. No reduced solver or assimilation algorithm is required in this opening activity. Those methods will provide tools to address the needs you identify today.

5. Before you leave

Explain the difference between a state, a quantity of interest and a measurement. Identify one reason a small residual for the model equations would not establish agreement with a physical system. Describe where a future assimilation step belongs in the model–observation loop.
