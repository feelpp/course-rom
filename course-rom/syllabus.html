Course map — 14 integrated sessions

M2 ROM & Data-Driven ROM · 28 contact hours · Cours intégré

The course introduces digital twins and develops numerical methods that can support their prediction and update processes. Lectures, short derivations, student notebook work and discussion share the same contact-time budget. Data assimilation is mandatory.

1. Prerequisites

Basic numerical linear algebra, PDE discretization, Python/NumPy, and elementary probability. The first experiment supplies the full-order solver; no Feel++ compilation is needed.

2. Learning outcomes

  • Explain the roles of models, observations and assimilation in a digital-twin scenario.

  • Construct and compare reduced spaces using POD and greedy selection.

  • Interpret a residual-based error estimate and its assumptions.

  • Explain and measure what EIM/DEIM changes in online evaluation.

  • Reconstruct states from observations using GEIM/PBDW and examine stability/noise.

  • Use KF/EnKF to combine forecasts and sequential observations, including ROM forecasts.

3. Teaching sequence

This is the proposed allocation within the confirmed 14-session budget. Dates beyond the opening session and assessment arrangements will be announced by the instructor.

Session Concept Practical objective

1

Digital twins and model/data roles

Vary a model parameter; examine an output and synthetic observations.

2

Galerkin reduction and POD

Build a snapshot space and compare full/reduced outputs.

3

A posteriori estimation

Compute a residual estimate and examine effectivity.

4

Greedy versus POD

Compare spaces on test cases and inspect online operations.

5

Non-affinity and EIM

Build and evaluate an empirical interpolant.

6

DEIM

Select evaluation points from a POD basis.

7

Hyper-reduction in practice

Compare held-out errors, conditioning and measured costs.

8

GEIM

Reconstruct from general sensor functionals.

9

PBDW

Combine a background approximation and a correction.

10

Noise and model discrepancy

Investigate sensor stability and reconstruction robustness.

11

Kalman filter

Implement forecast/update and vary covariance assumptions.

12

Ensemble Kalman filter

Compare ensemble sizes and forecast uncertainty.

13

ROM plus sequential assimilation

Compare forecast-only and assimilated thermal-model estimates.

14

Integrated experiment

Interpret accuracy, observations, assimilation and runtime together.

Practical tasks use selected cells from existing teaching notebooks, with reviewed versions released through session pages. Aim for approximately half of the total contact time in student practical activity, with the remainder devoted to explanations and discussion.

4. Course readings and reference material

The reference library has a broader scope than this course. Session pages identify required sections; other chapters and historical PDFs are not an implied additional reading list. SCM, extensive convergence proofs, geometric transformations, nonlinear certification, other filtering variants and learned surrogate models can be explored outside the selected route.

5. Practical work and assessment

For each practical, retain the notebook, the requested plots and short explanations of assumptions and results. Synthetic truth is available for evaluating experiments; it must not become an undeclared input to an estimation algorithm. Specific submissions and marking rules will be announced by the instructor.
