M2 ROM & Data-Driven ROM

How can a numerical model predict fast enough, assess its accuracy, and learn from measurements? This course develops methods useful for digital twins through lectures and hands-on notebook work.

Master CSMI · Université de Strasbourg · Christophe Prud’homme

1. Start here

  • Session 1 — Digital twins: models, observations and prediction

  • Thursday 10 September 2026, 13:30–15:30 (Europe/Paris).

  • Prepare your notebook environment and download the opening notebook.

2. A cours intégré

We have 14 sessions of 2 hours, combining mathematical explanation, guided computation and discussion. You will construct reduced approximations, examine their errors and costs, and combine models with observations. Data assimilation is a required part of the course.

Sessions Course progression

1–4

Digital-twin motivation; RB, a posteriori estimation, greedy and POD.

5–7

Non-affine problems; EIM and DEIM.

8–10

General measurements, GEIM and PBDW.

11–13

Sequential assimilation: KF, EnKF and ROM coupling.

14

Integrated experiment and synthesis.

See the course map and learning outcomes. Detailed session pages and practicals are released as they become available; only session 1 is part of this opening release.

3. One course within a broader reference

The Reduced Models & Digital Twins reference library holds reusable subject material. This M2 course selects readings and practical activities from it within the 28-hour timetable. Start with Digital twins: connecting models and observations.

Previous lecture PDFs and assignments remain available in the teaching archive. Their original years and deadlines do not define the current offering.
